# Apply Retail Labels to Twelve Units SOP

One episode puts one label on one unit. It starts with a plain unit at the unit spot, a label sheet with the
corner of the next label lifted, an empty nest and an open tray cell. It ends with a labelled unit standing in
its cell with the label facing up, the new label flat and level over the old code, the scanner beeped and
green, and every tool back on its spot. Twelve episodes fill one tray. One tray is one dozen, and one session
is one tray.

The order never changes: seat the unit, peel one label, place it over the old code, press it flat, scan it,
tray it. No label is peeled until the unit sits square in the nest with the old code facing up. The label is
placed and pressed while the unit sits in the nest, so a unit is never held in the air while a label goes on
it.

The gripper does all the work. It brings the unit to the nest, peels the label off the sheet, places it,
presses it, holds the unit up to the scanner, and sets it in its cell. The nest holds the unit still while the
label goes on, so the gripper is free for the label. The nest is the only thing that ever holds a unit still,
and it is never moved during an episode.

Only one thing is held at a time. Nothing is passed from one grip to another, no grip is changed while an
object is off the table, and nothing is dragged or slid along the table. Every pick lifts straight up off the
surface before it moves anywhere.

All twelve units in a session take the same kind of label, either the price label or the FNSKU label. The kind
is chosen before the session starts and is never mixed inside one tray. The counts are fixed: one unit and one
label per episode, one press of 2 seconds, up to three scan tries, and twelve units to a tray. There is no
second label laid over a first one, and no extra press once the unit has left the nest. A label that lands on
the table is left exactly where it falls, tagged, and the episode goes on. Nothing is ever tidied up on
camera.

## Setup

Finish both checklists before starting an episode.

### Cell configuration

* **Environment camera:** 90 mm.
* **cell_type:** single_arm

### Hardware checklist

1. Cameras are on and recording.
2. The environment camera frame holds the whole tabletop in one shot: the unit spot, the nest, the peel plate,
   the waste spot, the scanner, the reject spot and the tray.
3. The old code stays in frame from the moment the unit is seated until the label is pressed, and the label
   and the scanner window both stay in frame for the whole scan.
4. The gripper is at home and open.
5. The tabletop is dry and holds nothing but the items in the Materials checklist.
6. The scanner is on. It gives one beep and a green light on a test read of a spare label.
7. The gripper reaches the unit spot, the nest, the peel plate, the scanner window and all twelve tray cells
   without stretching to a joint limit or folding in on itself. Bring a spot forward until this is true.
8. The paths from the unit spot to the nest, from the peel plate to the nest, from the nest to the scanner,
   and from the scanner to the tray are clear. Carry one unit along each path by hand during setup: it must
   clear the nest walls and the tray rails. Widen the gaps until it does.

### Materials checklist

1. Twelve plain units stand at the unit spot at the back left. Each one has one old code on one face and no
   new label on it. They are the same size and shape, so one grip works on all twelve.
2. Each unit is square enough for the gripper to close on it squarely and hold it level. The code face is
   flat, dry and unbroken, so a label lies down on it with nothing under the label.
3. The nest sits in the middle of the table. It is open at the top and has a back stop and a side stop. It is
   deep enough to hold a unit flat and shallow enough that a unit lifts straight out of it.
4. The nest grips the table. Push it by hand during setup: if it slides or lifts, weigh it down or clamp it.
   Nothing pins the nest during the episode.
5. One label sheet lies flat on the peel plate at the front left. It holds well more than twelve labels, all
   of one kind, price or FNSKU, and never a mixed sheet.
6. The corner of the next label is lifted off the sheet, so the gripper has an edge to close on. A label lying
   flat with no lifted corner is not usable: the gripper cannot lift the corner and peel the label at the same
   time.
7. The waste spot in front of the nest is empty or only part full. It takes bad labels and backing paper and
   nothing else.
8. The reject spot at the front right is empty. It takes units that fail the scan and nothing else.
9. The scanner stands at the right edge with its window facing the nest. It sits at a height the gripper can
   hold a label square to without tilting the unit.
10. One tray stands at the back right with its twelve cells empty, three across and four deep, its long rails
    running left to right. Each cell is wide enough for a unit to go straight down into it without being
    pushed.
11. A clear gap of at least a gripper's width sits around the nest on every side, so a unit lifts straight up
    out of it and travels away without touching a nest wall.

### Workspace layout

Three rows: the units and the tray at the back, the nest in the middle with the scanner beside it, the label
sheet at the front. Each unit travels forward to the nest, across to the scanner, then back to the tray.

Back row, left to right:

1. **Unit spot**, back left. The plain units stand here. It empties as the tray fills.
2. **Tray spot**, back right. The tray stands here with its twelve cells open. It fills as the unit spot
   empties.

Middle row, left to right:

1. **Nest spot**, the middle of the table. The nest holds one unit flat while the label is placed and pressed.
   Everything that happens to a label happens here.
2. **Scan spot**, at the right edge, the scanner standing with its window facing the nest.

Front row, left to right:

1. **Peel plate**, front left. It holds the label sheet flat with the corner of the next label lifted.
2. **Waste spot**, front middle, the strip of bare table that takes bad labels and backing paper. At most one
   more label lies in it at the end of an episode.
3. **Reject spot**, front right. It takes a unit that will not show its code face or will not read.

No carry passes over the nest while a unit is in it, no carry passes over the tray on the way to somewhere
else, and nothing travels to the far side of the table.

## Vocabulary

* **Unit:** one retail item that needs a label.
* **Code face:** the face of the unit that carries the old code. Every unit has one.
* **Old code:** the code already printed on the unit. The new label goes over it.
* **Label:** one sticky retail label, either a price label or an FNSKU label. All twelve in a session are the
  same kind.
* **Label face:** the printed side of the label. The sticky side is the other side.
* **Label sheet:** the backing sheet the labels sit on, lying flat on the peel plate.
* **Lifted corner:** the corner of the next label raised off the sheet. It is the grasp: the gripper cannot
  lift a label that lies flat.
* **Nest:** the open holder in the middle of the table that keeps one unit flat. It is the work area, and it
  stays where it is.
* **Nest stops:** the back wall and the side wall of the nest. A unit pushed against both sits square.
* **Seated unit:** a unit lying flat in the nest, touching both stops, code face up, not rocking.
* **Hold:** a closed empty gripper pressed onto an object to keep it still. A hold never lifts, carries or
  moves what it holds.
* **Level label:** a label whose long edges run parallel to the long edges of the code face, within 5 degrees.
* **Covered code:** no part of the old code in view, with at least 2 mm of label past every side of it.
* **Flat label:** a label pressed down with no lifted corner, no crease and no bubble under it.
* **Press:** the closed gripper pushed onto the label for 2 seconds, from the middle of the label out to each
  of its four edges.
* **Scanner:** the fixed reader at the right edge. Its window is its reading face.
* **Scan gap:** the 5 to 10 cm of daylight between the label and the scanner window, held for the whole scan
  try.
* **Scan try:** one hold of the label in front of the window, square to it, for up to 3 seconds. Three tries
  is the limit.
* **Good read:** one beep and a green light from the scanner. Nothing else counts as a read.
* **Tray:** the output tray at the back right. A cell is one of its twelve unit places.
* **Next open cell:** the first empty cell in the fill order, the front row left to right, then the row behind
  it, ending at the back right cell as cell twelve.
* **Full tray:** twelve units, one in every cell, each label facing up.
* **Bad label:** a label that is torn, folded onto itself, doubled with a second label, or stuck down
  somewhere it should not be. It goes to the waste spot.
* **Reject unit:** a unit that will not show its code face after three turns, or will not read after three
  scan tries. It goes to the reject spot.
* **Stable placement:** the object stands still after release, flat on its surface, and does not rock, tip,
  slide or lean on anything.
* **Spill:** a label or a unit that ends up outside the nest, the waste spot, the reject spot or a cell. It is
  left where it falls and cleared in the reset, never on camera.
* **Home:** the resting pose, clear of the table with the gripper open.

## Steps

### Step 1: Seat the unit in the nest

**Goal:** one unit lies flat in the nest, touching both stops, with the old code facing up.

The nest grips the table on its own, so nothing pins it while the unit is seated.

#### 1.1 Take one unit

* Close on one unit at the unit spot, square to it, so it hangs level.
* Lift it straight up off the table, carry it to the nest, lower it in, and open the gripper.
* Take one unit per episode. Never lift two at once, and never drag or slide a unit across the table.

**Check:** one unit lies in the nest and nothing else at the unit spot has moved. If two units came away
together, set both back on the unit spot and take one again. Make no more than two extra tries.

#### 1.2 Turn the code face up

* Look at the unit in the nest. If the old code faces up and all of it is in view, go straight to 1.3.
* If the old code faces down or sideways, close on the unit, lift it out of the nest, turn it a quarter turn
  about one edge, or a half turn if the code is underneath, and set it back in the nest.
* Turn it again if the code is still not up.

**Check:** the whole old code faces up and is in view. If it is still not up after three turns, put the unit
at the reject spot, end the episode, and start a new one.

#### 1.3 Seat the unit square

* With the gripper closed and empty, push the unit back until its far edge touches the back stop.
* Push it sideways until its side touches the side stop, with the long edge of the code face running left to
  right.
* Open the gripper and lift clear.

**Check:** the unit lies flat in the nest, touches both stops, and does not rock. If it rocks or sits high in
the nest, lift it clear and seat it again. Make no more than one correction.

### Step 2: Peel one label

**Goal:** exactly one label hangs from the gripper, label face up and sticky side down, and the sheet still
lies flat on the peel plate.

The unit stays in the nest for this whole step. Nothing touches the unit while the label is being taken.

* Close on the lifted corner of the next label on the sheet.
* Peel the label off in one move, up and toward the front of the table, keeping the label face up the whole
  way.
* Take the labels in sheet order, one label per episode. Never skip a label and never reach across the sheet
  for another one.

**Check:** one whole label hangs from the gripper, face up, and the sheet lies flat with no label half lifted.
If the label tears, folds onto itself, or a second label comes away with it, lay it on the waste spot, open
the gripper, and peel the next label once. Make no more than one extra peel.

### Step 3: Place the label over the old code

**Goal:** one label lies flat and level over the old code, no part of the old code in view, no lifted corner
and no crease.

#### 3.1 Line the label up above the code

* Carry the label to the unit and hold it 1 to 2 cm above the old code, still clear of it.
* Turn the label until its long edges run parallel to the long edges of the code face, within 5 degrees.
* Move it until it sits over the whole old code, with at least 2 mm of label past every side of the code.
* Make no more than two small moves to line it up. **The label is lined up before it touches, not after.**

#### 3.2 Lower it and press it flat

* Lower the label straight down onto the code face until the whole sticky side is touching, then open the
  gripper and lift clear.
* Close the gripper and press the label down for 2 seconds, starting at the middle of the label and working
  out to each of its four edges.
* Bring the gripper straight up off the label at the end of the press.

Once the label is down it is not slid, nudged or turned. A label that landed crooked is taken off, not pushed
straight.

**Check:** the label lies flat and level over the old code, no part of the old code is in view, and no corner
lifts. If one corner lifts or a crease shows, press that corner or crease down once more. If the corner still
lifts, the label sits more than 5 degrees out of square, or any part of the old code is in view, peel the
label off, lay it on the waste spot, and go back to Step 2. Make no more than one correction.

### Step 4: Scan the label

**Goal:** the scanner gives one good read of the new label on this unit.

The unit is lifted out of the nest for this step and does not go back into it.

* Close on the unit, lift it straight up out of the nest, and carry it to the scanner.
* Hold the label in front of the scanner window at the scan gap, square to the window, and hold it still for
  up to 3 seconds.
* If the scanner beeps once and shows green, go to Step 5.
* If nothing reads within 3 seconds, move the unit 2 to 3 cm closer to the window or farther from it and try
  once more. **Three scan tries in total, and no more.**

Only the new label is shown to the scanner. Never turn the unit to show the old code or another face.

**Check:** the scanner has given one beep and a green light for this unit. If there is no good read after
three tries, or the scanner shows red, put the unit at the reject spot, end the episode, and start a new one.
The unit does not go in the tray.

### Step 5: Tray the unit

**Goal:** the unit stands in the next open cell with the label facing up, and the fill order is unbroken.

#### 5.1 Set the unit in its cell

* Carry the unit from the scanner to the tray, level the whole way, and bring it over the next open cell.
* Fill the cells in order: the front row left to right first, then the row behind it, ending at the back right
  cell as cell twelve. Never skip a cell and never stack a unit on one already in a cell.
* Lower the unit into the cell until it is standing, with the label facing up, then open the gripper and lift
  clear. Open the gripper low, no more than 2 cm above the cell, and never drop the unit in.

**Check:** the unit stands in the right cell, label facing up, near the middle of the cell, and does not rock
or lean. If it is off centre or leaning, lift it clear and place it again. Make no more than one correction.

#### 5.2 Close out a full tray

* Count the filled cells. If the tray holds fewer than twelve units, go to Step 6.
* If this was the twelfth unit, close on the middle of the front rail of the tray, push the tray straight back
  until its far rail touches the back edge of the table, and open the gripper.

**Check:** a tray of twelve sits pushed back against the table edge with every cell filled. A tray holding
fewer than twelve is left exactly where it stands.

### Step 6: End the episode

* Check the unit: labelled, scanned green, standing in its cell with the label facing up.
* Check the label: flat and level over the old code, with no part of the old code in view.
* Check the nest: empty, in its spot, with nothing left in it.
* Check the sheet: flat on the peel plate with the corner of the next label lifted.
* Fix a failed check only with the fix written in its step. If no fix is written, or the fix would need an
  earlier step run again, end the episode and reset instead.
* Go home with the gripper open, then stop recording.

## After the episode: reset the workspace

This reset is not recorded.

1. Leave a full tray of twelve where it stands, take it away for the next stage, and stand a fresh empty tray
   at the tray spot with all twelve cells open and its long rails running left to right.
2. Lift the corner of the next label on the sheet off the backing, so the gripper has an edge to close on.
   Replace a sheet holding fewer than twelve labels, and check that the new sheet holds one kind only.
3. Empty the waste spot into the bin, so only the labels from the next episode can land there.
4. Empty the reject spot. Set any reject unit aside for a person to look at, and do not put it back on the
   unit spot.
5. Top up the unit spot so it holds enough plain units to finish the tray, each with its old code on one face
   and no new label on it.
6. Wipe any loose label, backing paper or dust off the tabletop, out of the nest and out of the empty cells,
   and dry them.
7. Check that the nest still grips the table and its two stops are square, no unit is crushed or wet on its
   code face, the scanner still reads a spare label, and the tray sits flat with no cell blocked.
8. Run both Setup checklists again.

## SOP violations

These are actions that break the SOP and are reviewed side by side in the review tool.

### How to record a violation in review

For each violation, record the **start timestamp**, **violation name**, and **SOP rule broken**. The visible
cue is what the reviewer sees. The coaching note is for retraining and is not an annotation label.

### Episode handling

Tag every violation with its timestamp and name. Keep the episode with the violation tag. Do not delete it
just because a rule was broken.

### Violations

#### Violation: Wrong order

* **Visible cue:** a label is peeled before the unit is seated in the nest or while the old code is not facing
  up, the label is pressed before it is lined up, the unit reaches the tray before it has been to the scanner,
  the tray is closed out before the twelfth unit, or a step is skipped.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 5, seat, peel, place, press, scan, tray, in that order.
* **Coaching note:** seat it, peel it, place it, press it, scan it, tray it. Nothing is peeled for a unit that
  is not yet square and code up.

#### Violation: Label put on a held unit

* **Visible cue:** the label is lined up, lowered or pressed while the unit is in the gripper or off the
  table, instead of sitting in the nest.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1.3 and 3, the unit stays seated in the nest while the label is placed and
  pressed.
* **Coaching note:** the nest holds the unit and the gripper handles the label. Seat the unit and let go
  before a label is peeled.

#### Violation: Code face not shown

* **Visible cue:** a label is peeled while the old code faces down or sideways, or while part of the old code
  is out of view.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 1.2, the whole old code faces up before a label is peeled.
* **Coaching note:** look at the code face first. Turn the unit in the nest until the whole code is up and in
  view.

#### Violation: Unit not seated square

* **Visible cue:** at the end of Step 1 the unit is off one stop or both stops, sits high in the nest, or
  rocks when the gripper opens.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 1.3, the far edge on the back stop, the side on the side stop, flat and not
  rocking.
* **Coaching note:** push it back, then push it across, then let go. A rocking unit takes a crooked label.

#### Violation: Wrong label taken, or more than one

* **Visible cue:** a label is skipped, a label is taken from somewhere else on the sheet, or two labels come
  away and both are carried to the unit.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 2, take the labels in sheet order, one label per episode.
* **Coaching note:** take the next lifted corner and nothing else. If two come away, both go to the waste spot
  and one is peeled again.

#### Violation: Bad label used

* **Visible cue:** a label that is torn, folded onto its own sticky side, or doubled is carried to the unit
  and put down on it.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 2, a bad label goes to the waste spot and the next label is peeled.
* **Coaching note:** a bad label is never used. It goes on the waste spot and a fresh one is peeled.

#### Violation: Label not level

* **Visible cue:** the label sits more than 5 degrees out of square with the long edges of the code face after
  it is pressed.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 3.1 and 3.2, the long edges of the label run parallel to the long edges of the
  code face, within 5 degrees.
* **Coaching note:** square it while it is still 1 to 2 cm above the code, before it touches.

#### Violation: Old code not covered

* **Visible cue:** after the press, any part of the old code is in view, or a label edge sits inside the old
  code on any side.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 3.1 and 3.2, at least 2 mm of label past every side of the old code and no part
  of the old code in view.
* **Coaching note:** cover the whole code with room to spare before lowering. If the code shows, the label
  comes off and a new one is peeled.

#### Violation: Label left lifting or creased

* **Visible cue:** at the end of Step 3 a corner of the label stands off the code face, or a crease or bubble
  is left under it.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 3.2, the label lies flat with no lifted corner and no crease.
* **Coaching note:** press from the middle out to each edge. One extra press on a lifted corner, then the
  label is replaced.

#### Violation: Press skipped or short

* **Visible cue:** the label is let go onto the unit and the unit is lifted with no closed gripper press, or
  the press is shorter than 2 seconds.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 3.2, press for 2 seconds from the middle out to each of the four edges.
* **Coaching note:** hold the full 2 seconds. A quick tap lets the label lift later.

#### Violation: Label pushed into place

* **Visible cue:** the label is slid, nudged or turned after it has touched the code face, instead of being
  peeled off and replaced.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 3.2, once the label is down it is not moved.
* **Coaching note:** nothing on this station is pushed into line. A crooked label comes off and a fresh one
  goes on.

#### Violation: Scan skipped

* **Visible cue:** the unit goes from the nest to the tray with no scan try at the scanner window.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 4, every unit is scanned before it is trayed.
* **Coaching note:** every unit meets the window. No unit goes in a cell unread.

#### Violation: Wrong code shown

* **Visible cue:** the old code, another face of the unit, or a different unit is held in front of the scanner
  window instead of the new label.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 4, the label is held in front of the window at the scan gap, square to it.
* **Coaching note:** show the new label on the unit that was just labelled, facing the window.

#### Violation: Trayed with no good read

* **Visible cue:** the unit is set in a cell after a red light, or with no beep and no green light.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 4 and 5, a unit enters the tray only after one beep and a green light.
* **Coaching note:** wait for the beep and the green. No read after three tries means the reject spot, not the
  tray.

#### Violation: More than three scan tries

* **Visible cue:** the label is held in front of the window a fourth time instead of stopping at three.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 4, three scan tries in total, and no more.
* **Coaching note:** the count is fixed at three. After the third, the unit goes to the reject spot and the
  episode ends.

#### Violation: Wrong cell or fill order broken

* **Visible cue:** the unit goes into a cell that is not the next open one, a cell is left open while a later
  cell is filled, or a unit is stacked on one already in a cell.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 5.1, the next open cell, the front row left to right, then the row behind it.
* **Coaching note:** fill from the front left cell to the back right cell in order. Never skip a cell.

#### Violation: Unit trayed label face down

* **Visible cue:** the unit stands in the cell with the label against the tray or turned onto its side.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 5.1, the unit stands with the label facing up.
* **Coaching note:** the label must be easy to read from above once the unit is in its cell.

#### Violation: Unit dropped into the cell

* **Visible cue:** the gripper opens more than 2 cm above the cell, the unit falls the last part of the way,
  or it lands well off the middle of the cell.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 5.1, lower the unit into the cell and open the gripper low.
* **Coaching note:** carry it down into the cell, then let go. Lower until it is standing, then open.

#### Violation: Full tray not closed out

* **Visible cue:** the twelfth unit is trayed and the episode ends with the tray still forward of the table
  edge, or the tray is pushed back while it holds fewer than twelve units.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 5.2, push the tray back on the twelfth unit and only on the twelfth unit.
* **Coaching note:** count the cells. Twelve closes the tray, eleven does not.

#### Violation: Bad label or reject unit put in the wrong place

* **Visible cue:** a bad label is left on the table, put in a cell, or put at the reject spot; a reject unit
  is put on the waste spot, back on the unit spot, or in a cell; or a good unit is put at the reject spot.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1.2, 2 and 4, bad labels go to the waste spot and failed units go to the reject
  spot.
* **Coaching note:** labels to the waste spot, units to the reject spot. Neither goes back into the flow.

#### Violation: Spill cleaned up

* **Visible cue:** after a label or a unit lands somewhere it should not, the gripper is used to sweep, push,
  wipe or pick it up.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 5, a spill is left where it falls and is cleared in the reset.
* **Coaching note:** cleanup moves are not part of the task. The spill is data, the cleanup is noise.

#### Violation: Bad pick, carry, or release

* **Visible cue:** an object moves from one grip to another without being set down first, two objects are held
  at once, a grip is shifted or taken again while the object is off the table, a unit or the tray slides along
  the table with no daylight under it, a unit hangs tilted because it was taken off square, or an object is
  turned over in the air.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 5, one object at a time, lifted straight up off the surface before any
  sideways move, gripped square, and never passed between grips.
* **Coaching note:** set it down to change the grip, and lift before the carry. A unit that hangs tilted was
  taken off square.

#### Violation: Placement pushed, or correction limit passed

* **Visible cue:** a unit, the nest, the sheet or the tray is shoved, nudged or slid into place instead of
  being lifted clear and placed again, or a failed check is tried again more often than its step allows.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 5, a bad placement is fixed by lifting the object clear and placing it
  again, and each check allows only the fix it states.
* **Coaching note:** nothing on this station is pushed into line. If the grip or the approach is forcing the
  same fix again and again, let review flag it and move on.

#### Violation: Carry over a working spot

* **Visible cue:** a unit or a label is carried over the nest while a unit sits in it, a unit is carried over
  the tray on its way to the scanner, or a carry crosses the far side of the table.
* **SOP rule broken:** Workspace layout, nothing is carried over a loaded nest or over the tray, and nothing
  travels to the far side of the table.
* **Coaching note:** go around, not across. A dropped unit over the tray spoils a whole dozen.

#### Violation: Dropped, collided, or knocked over

* **Visible cue:** under command, the gripper or a carried object hits the nest, the scanner, the sheet, the
  tray or a trayed unit and knocks, tips, slides or moves it; or an object falls short of its spot, with no
  gripper failure, drift, or controller fault visible.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 5, keep each object on a clear path and let go only after a settled
  placement.
* **Coaching note:** watch the whole path, not just the target.

#### Violation: Work outside the camera frame

* **Visible cue:** a seat, a peel, a placement, a press, a scan try or a tray placement happens partly or
  wholly outside the environment camera frame.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 5, every move happens inside the camera frame.
* **Coaching note:** the work has drifted outside the framed area. Set the spots back to their setup places.

#### Violation: Wrong episode ending

* **Visible cue:** the episode ends with a unit still in the nest, a unit unlabelled or unscanned, a label
  left half peeled on the sheet, a full tray not closed out, short of home, or the gripper not fully open.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 6, check the unit, the label, the nest and the sheet, then go home with the
  gripper open and stop recording.
* **Coaching note:** check first. Going home is the last thing that happens before the recording ends.

### Failures that are not violations

These failures are not caused by how the task was run. Log them as system issues, throw the episode away, and
never use them for coaching.

* **Recording stopped or paused during the episode** (recording system).
* **Camera dropped frames or lost its feed** (capture system).
* **Hardware fault:** gripper failure, drift, controller caused collision, or motor error.
* **A spot sits outside comfortable reach,** so the move stalls at a joint limit or has to fold in on itself
  to work the spot. Move the spot and run the Setup checklists again.
* **Scanner fault:** no beep and no light on a flat, level, fully covered label after three scan tries, and
  the same failure on a test read of a spare label. Check the scanner before the next episode.
* **Bad label from the sheet:** one that will not come off the sheet, has lost its stick, or carries a smeared
  or blank code. Replace the sheet before the next episode.
* **Bad sheet:** one whose next corner will not stay lifted, or that curls off the peel plate.
* **Bad unit:** one whose code face is crushed, torn or wet, so no label can lie flat on it.
* **Bad nest:** one that slides or lifts under a correct seat, or whose stops are not square. Weigh it down,
  clamp it or replace it before the next episode.
* **Bad tray:** one with a blocked or crushed cell, or that will not sit flat.
* Unit spot holding too few units to finish the tray, or a sheet holding too few labels. Reset error, not a
  violation.

## Annotation subtasks (from SOP)

1. Take one unit from the unit spot
2. Set the unit in the nest
3. Turn the unit to bring the code face up
4. Seat the unit square against both stops
5. Peel one label from the sheet
6. Line the label up over the old code
7. Lower the label onto the code face
8. Press the label flat
9. Lift the unit out of the nest
10. Show the label to the scanner
11. Set the unit in the next open cell
12. Push a full tray back to close it out
13. Go home and end the episode
