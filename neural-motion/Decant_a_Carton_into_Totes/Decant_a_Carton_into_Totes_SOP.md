# Decant a Carton into Totes SOP

One episode decants one carton into two totes. The dunnage comes off the top, the two upright units
go into the left tote, the two flat units go into the right tote, each tote is counted against its
own label card and then labeled, the base pad comes out of the empty carton, and the carton is
folded flat on the desk where it stood.

The order never changes: air pillow, fill sheet, upright units, count and label the left tote, flat
units, count and label the right tote, base pad, flatten. No unit is lifted until both top pieces of
dunnage are in the dunnage bin, no label card is placed until its tote has been counted, and the
carton is not flattened until it is bare.

With the carton at back center, the left gripper works the dunnage, the carton's left half, the left
tote, and card A, and the right gripper works the carton's right half, the right tote, and card B. Both
grippers press the carton flat together in the last step. Neither gripper crosses the middle.

The carton is never lifted, slid, tipped, or turned while it holds anything, and the totes and the
dunnage bin stay where they are put. Nothing is turned in the air, no unit is pulled out from between
its neighbors, nothing is stacked or wedged in a tote, and no gripper opens anywhere but over the spot
the thing in it is going into.

The desk is set up in one of three ways. Only the carton moves; the two label cards, the two totes, and
the dunnage bin are in the same place in all three.

* **Config L:** the carton is at the back-left, just left of the middle, with its right wall on the midline.
* **Config M:** the carton is at back center, straddling the midline.
* **Config R:** the carton is at the back-right, just right of the middle, with its left wall on the midline.

Where a step depends on the setup it says so on an **IF** line — look at the desk and follow the line
that matches.

What stays constant across all sessions:

* **Start position:** the carton starts at the back-left (**Config L**), back center (**Config M**), or
  the back-right (**Config R**). One config per episode, chosen before recording and never changed
  mid-episode.
* **Same-side rule:** the gripper on the carton's side takes everything out of it — the **left gripper**
  in Config L, the **right gripper** in Config R. In Config M the carton straddles the midline and each
  gripper takes its own half. No arm reaches across the desk into the carton.
* **Hand-over rule:** a unit whose tote is on the other side of the desk **changes hands** above the
  **dunnage bin**: the taking gripper holds it still, the other gripper closes on the opposite side of
  it, and only then does the taking gripper open and lift clear. In Config L that is each flat unit,
  left gripper to right gripper; in Config R each upright unit, right gripper to left gripper. Nothing
  is handed over in Config M.
* **Fixed roles:** the left gripper labels the left tote with card A and the right gripper labels the
  right tote with card B; every piece of dunnage goes to the dunnage bin from whichever gripper took it;
  both grippers press the carton flat in Step 7, each on the wall on its own side, which in Config L and
  R puts the far gripper on the wall standing at the midline; and the order above never changes.

## Setup

Complete both checklists before starting an episode.

### Cell configuration

* **Environment camera:** 900 mm.
* **cell_type:** bimanual

### Hardware checklist

1. Cameras are on and recording.
2. The environment camera shows the whole desk: the carton in the start zone for this episode's
   config, card A at the back left,
   card B at the back right, the dunnage bin at front center, the left tote at the front left, and
   the right tote at the front right.
3. The carton interior is visible from above so every lift can be seen leaving it, and the floor of
   both totes is visible so the units in each one can be counted from the recording.
4. Both arms are at home with grippers open.
5. The desk is bare apart from the carton, the two totes, the dunnage bin, and the two label cards.
   There are no marks, trays, or fixtures on it.
6. Nothing obstructs the paths between the carton and the front of the desk. The left arm reaches
   the carton's left half, card A, the dunnage bin, the left tote, and (Config L) the whole carton. The
   right arm reaches the carton's right half, card B, the right tote, and (Config R) the whole carton
   and the dunnage bin.

### Materials checklist

1. One **open top carton** sitting square on the desk in the start zone for this episode's config,
   base flat on the desk, with clear desk on all four sides. It has four walls and no top flaps. Its
   base flaps are folded flat under it, neither tucked into each other nor taped, so the carton folds
   flat along its corner creases under an even inward press.
   * **Config L:** back-left, just left of the middle, its right wall on the midline, card A clear to
     its left
   * **Config M:** back center, straddling the midline
   * **Config R:** back-right, just right of the middle, its left wall on the midline, card B clear to
     its right
2. Four units lie in the carton in one layer: two identical **upright units** (SKU A) standing on
   their bases in the left half, and two identical **flat units** (SKU B) lying flat in the right
   half. There is a clear gap between neighbors so a gripper closes on one without touching another,
   and nothing rests on top of anything else.
3. An **upright unit** stands on its own base without support, keeps its shape when a gripper closes
   around its side wall, and is taller than it is wide, so standing and toppled are told apart in
   frame. A **flat unit** lies flat on a face without rocking, is rigid across its thickness so the
   gripper takes both side walls without squashing it, and is wider than it is tall. The two SKUs
   are told apart at a glance, and the two units within a SKU are identical to each other. No unit
   rolls when it is set down, folds or deforms under the grasp, or stands tall enough that the
   gripper touches a tote rim on the way in.
4. One **base pad**, a flat card covering the carton floor, lies under all four units.
5. One **fill sheet** of crumpled paper lies spread over the four units, and one **air pillow**,
   inflated and sealed, lies loose on top of the fill sheet.
6. Two open top **totes**, empty and dry, walls low enough that a unit is lowered in from above
   without the gripper touching the rim, each with a flat **label ledge** along the top of its near
   wall. The left tote sits at the front left and the right tote at the front right, both square to
   the front edge.
7. Two printed **label cards**, stiff enough to keep their shape when pinched at an edge. Card A
   carries the SKU A name and the number two and lies printed side up at the back left. Card B
   carries the SKU B name and the number two and lies printed side up at the back right.
8. One **dunnage bin**, open top and empty, at front center between the two totes, with its rim
   clear of both totes.

### Workspace layout

Every zone is a position on the bare desk. Nothing is marked, so each position is held by where the
container was set.

* **Carton (start zone):** at the back of the desk — back-left, just left of the middle (**Config L**),
  back center, straddling the midline (**Config M**), or back-right, just right of the middle
  (**Config R**) — close enough to the middle that the arm on its side reaches all of it and the other
  arm reaches the wall standing at the midline without leaning out. It stays on the desk for the whole
  episode.
* **Card A spot:** the back left corner, left of the carton and clear of it.
* **Card B spot:** the back right corner, right of the carton and clear of it.
* **Left tote:** the front left of the desk, in line with the carton's left half.
* **Right tote:** the front right of the desk, in line with the carton's right half.
* **Dunnage bin:** front center, between the two totes and in front of the carton, clear of both
  totes.

SKU A is packed in the carton's left half and goes to the left tote. SKU B is packed in the right half
and goes to the right tote. No gripper carries a unit across the midline — in Config L and R the unit
that must cross changes hands above the dunnage bin — and no carried unit passes over a tote it is not
going into.

## Vocabulary

* **Arm assignment:** the left arm works all three pieces of dunnage, the carton's left half, card
  A, and the left tote. The right arm works the carton's right half, card B, and the right tote.
  Both arms press the carton flat in Step 8. Neither arm takes the other's zones. In Config L the left
  arm also takes the flat units out of the carton and hands them to the right arm above the dunnage
  bin; in Config R the right arm also takes the dunnage and the upright units out, puts the dunnage in
  the bin itself, and hands each upright unit to the left arm above the bin.
* **Start zone:** where the carton stands for the whole episode — back-left, just left of the middle
  (**Config L**), back center (**Config M**), or back-right, just right of the middle (**Config R**).
  One per episode, chosen before recording and never changed mid-episode.
* **Unit:** one sellable item of a SKU. SKU A is the two upright units, SKU B is the two flat
  units. The two units of a SKU are identical, so any one of them satisfies that SKU.
* **Dunnage:** the packing material that protects the units and is never a unit. There are exactly
  three pieces: the **air pillow** on top, the **fill sheet** spread over the units under it, and
  the **base pad** flat on the carton floor beneath them. All three go in the dunnage bin and none
  of them ever goes in a tote.
* **Label card:** the printed card naming a SKU and the number of units its tote must hold. It is
  read before the count and placed after it. Card A belongs to the left tote and card B to the right
  tote.
* **Label ledge:** the flat top of a tote's near wall, where the label card rests printed side up,
  square to the front edge, clear of the tote's opening.
* **Unit grasps:** an upright unit around its side wall, kept upright; a flat unit across its two
  side walls, taking the full thickness. The air pillow across its middle, the fill sheet at its
  near edge, the base pad at its near edge. The sheet and the pad both span the whole carton, so
  the near edge is pinched at a point in the half of the carton on the taking gripper's side — the
  left half for the left gripper, the right half for the right gripper.
* **Lift:** taking a unit or a piece of dunnage straight down onto its grasp, closing, and raising
  it straight up until there is daylight under it, before anything moves sideways.
* **Lower in:** carrying a unit level over the tote, lowering it straight down until it is resting
  on the tote floor clear of what is already in, and releasing once it is resting. A unit is never
  dropped in from above the rim.
* **One layer:** every unit in a tote resting on the tote floor in the orientation it was packed in,
  none stacked on another, none leaning on a wall, and none wedged against another. The upright
  units stand on their bases and the flat units lie flat.
* **Settled:** the thing stays put for 2 seconds after the gripper lifts clear, with nothing
  rocking, sliding, tipping, or rolling.
* **Count hold:** both grippers clear of a tote and out of the way, the tote and its label card both
  in frame, held still for 2 seconds so the units in the tote can be counted from the recording.
* **Release point:** over the dunnage bin, over the tote, or over the label ledge the thing in the
  gripper is going to, or, in a hand-over, once the other gripper has closed on the unit. A gripper
  opens nowhere else.
* **Hand over:** one gripper holds a unit still above the dunnage bin, the other gripper closes on the
  opposite side of it, and only then does the first gripper open and lift clear. Config L (flat units,
  left to right) and Config R (upright units, right to left) only, because the unit's tote is on the
  far side of the desk.
* **Flat:** the carton lying folded on the desk with no wall standing, its base flaps splayed out
  flat, nothing trapped under it, and no part of it springing back up when the grippers lift clear.

## Steps

Steps 1 to 8 are one carton. Every unit is lifted straight up out of the carton and carried in the
orientation it will be set down in. Steps 1, 2, 4, and 6 depend on where the carton is: in Config L the
**left gripper** takes everything out of it and hands each flat unit to the right gripper above the
dunnage bin; in Config R the **right gripper** takes everything out and hands each upright unit to the
left gripper above the bin; in Config M each gripper takes its own half. Every other line is the same in
all three configs.

### Step 1: Clear the top dunnage

**Goal:** the air pillow and the fill sheet in the dunnage bin and all four units in view.

Look where the carton is before reaching for the air pillow.

* **IF the carton is at the back-left (Config L):** the **left gripper** closes on the **air pillow**
  across its middle, lifts it straight up, carries it level forward and right to the **dunnage bin**,
  lowers it in until it is resting, and releases.
* **IF the carton is at back center (Config M):** the **left gripper** closes on the **air pillow**
  across its middle, lifts it straight up, carries it level straight forward to the **dunnage bin**,
  lowers it in until it is resting, and releases.
* **IF the carton is at the back-right (Config R):** the **right gripper** closes on the **air pillow**
  across its middle, lifts it straight up, carries it level forward and left to the **dunnage bin**,
  lowers it in until it is resting, and releases.

Then, in all three:

* The same gripper then pinches the near edge of the **fill sheet** at a point in its own half of the
  carton, lifts it straight up out of the carton, carries it level to the dunnage bin, lowers it in,
  and releases.
* Take the sheet in one piece. Never tear it, and never push it down the side of the carton to get
  it out of the way.

**Check:** the dunnage bin holds the air pillow and the fill sheet, all four units are uncovered and
in view, two upright units in the left half and two flat units in the right half, and nothing has
been pulled out of the carton with the sheet. If a unit comes up with the sheet, lower it back into
the carton in its own half before going on.

### Step 2: Decant SKU A into the left tote

**Goal:** the carton's left half empty and both upright units resting in one layer in the left tote.

* **IF Config L or M:** the **left gripper** closes on an **upright unit** around its side wall, lifts
  it straight up out of the carton, carries it level to the **left tote**, lowers it in until it is
  resting on the tote floor, and releases. **IF Config R:** the **right gripper** closes on an
  **upright unit** around its side wall, lifts it straight up out of the carton, carries it level to
  above the **dunnage bin**, and holds it still; the **left gripper** closes around the opposite side
  wall, the right gripper opens and lifts clear, and the left gripper carries it level to the **left
  tote**, lowers it in until it is resting on the tote floor, and releases.
* Take the second **upright unit** the same way once the first is resting and settled, and lower it
  onto clear tote floor rather than onto or against the upright unit already in.

Take each upright unit straight up. Never slide one out from between its neighbors, never move one
unit aside to reach another, and never press the upright units down into the tote to make room.

**Check:** both upright units rest in the left tote in one layer, standing on their bases, none
stacked, leaning, or wedged, and the carton's left half is bare down to the base pad. If an upright
unit has landed on top of another, lift it clear and lower it onto open tote floor.

### Step 3: Count SKU A and label the left tote

**Goal:** the left tote counted against card A and card A resting on its label ledge.

* Read the number printed on **card A** and count the upright units in the **left tote**.
* Hold both grippers clear of the tote for the **count hold** with the tote and card A both in
  frame.
* The **left gripper** pinches the near edge of **card A**, lifts it straight up, carries it level
  to the left tote, lowers it flat onto the **label ledge** printed side up and square to the front
  edge, and releases once it is resting.

The card goes down only after the count matches. If the tote holds any number of upright units other
than the number on card A, leave the card where it is and end the episode as a count mismatch (see
Failures that are not violations).

**Check:** the count in the tote matches the number on card A, the card lies flat and printed side
up on the label ledge, readable in frame, and it is clear of the tote's opening and not resting on
an upright unit.

### Step 4: Decant SKU B into the right tote

**Goal:** the carton's right half empty and both flat units resting in one layer in the right tote.

* **IF Config M or R:** the **right gripper** closes on a **flat unit** across its two side walls,
  lifts it straight up out of the carton, carries it level to the **right tote**, lowers it in until
  it is resting flat on the tote floor, and releases. **IF Config L:** the **left gripper** closes on
  a **flat unit** across its two side walls, lifts it straight up out of the carton, carries it level
  to above the **dunnage bin**, and holds it still; the **right gripper** closes across its other two
  side walls, the left gripper opens and lifts clear, and the right gripper carries it level to the
  **right tote**, lowers it in until it is resting flat on the tote floor, and releases.
* Take the second **flat unit** the same way once the first is resting and settled, and lower it
  flat onto clear tote floor.

Never tip or tilt the carton to bring a flat unit within reach. If a flat unit sits deep in the
carton, come down on it from above.

**Check:** both flat units rest flat in the right tote in one layer, none stacked, standing on edge,
or leaning on a wall, and the carton's right half is bare down to the base pad. If a flat unit has
landed on another, lift it clear and lower it onto open tote floor.

### Step 5: Count SKU B and label the right tote

**Goal:** the right tote counted against card B and card B resting on its label ledge.

* Read the number printed on **card B** and count the flat units in the **right tote**.
* Hold both grippers clear of the tote for the **count hold** with the tote and card B both in
  frame.
* The **right gripper** pinches the near edge of **card B**, lifts it straight up, carries it level
  to the right tote, lowers it flat onto the **label ledge** printed side up and square to the front
  edge, and releases once it is resting.

The card goes down only after the count matches, the same way as card A.

**Check:** the count in the tote matches the number on card B, the card lies flat and printed side
up on the label ledge, readable in frame, and it is clear of the tote's opening and not resting on a
flat unit.

### Step 6: Clear the base pad

**Goal:** the carton bare and the base pad in the dunnage bin.

* **IF Config L or M:** the **left gripper** pinches the near edge of the **base pad** at a point in
  the carton's left half, lifts it straight up out of the carton, carries it level to the **dunnage
  bin**, lowers it in, and releases. **IF Config R:** the **right gripper** pinches the near edge of
  the **base pad** at a point in the carton's right half and does the same.

The pad covers the whole carton floor, and it comes up whole from that one pinch in the gripper's own
half. The gripper never reaches past the midline to take the pad from the other half, and the pad is
never taken by a corner, folded, or pulled out in pieces.

**Check:** the carton floor is bare from front to back and wall to wall, the dunnage bin holds all three
pieces of dunnage, and nothing has come out of the carton with the pad. If a unit is still in the carton,
put it in its own tote and count that tote again before going on.

### Step 7: Flatten the carton

**Goal:** the empty carton folded flat on the desk where it stood.

* The **left gripper**, closed, comes down on the outside of the carton's **left wall** at its
  middle, and the **right gripper**, closed, comes down on the outside of the **right wall** at its
  middle, both level with the middle of the wall.
* Press both walls toward each other in one even motion until the carton folds along its corner
  creases and lies flat on the desk.
* Hold both grippers on the folded carton for 2 seconds so the creases set, then lift both straight
  up clear of it.

Both grippers press together so the carton folds in place instead of being shoved across the desk. Never
flatten it with one gripper, never press on the rim of a single wall to tip it over, and never lift the
carton off the desk to fold it in the air.

**Check:** the carton lies flat where it stood, no wall standing, base flaps splayed out flat, nothing
trapped under it, and it stays flat when the grippers lift clear. If a wall springs back up, press it
down again with the gripper on its own side.

### Step 8: Confirm the decant and end the episode

* Confirm the left tote holds its two upright units with card A on its ledge, the right tote holds
  its two flat units with card B on its ledge, the dunnage bin holds all three pieces of dunnage,
  and the carton is flat.
* Return both arms home, clear of the carton, the totes, and the bin, then stop recording.

## After the episode: reset the workspace

This reset is not recorded.

1. Lift the two label cards off the ledges and lay them printed side up at their spots, card A at
   the back left and card B at the back right.
2. Empty both totes and set the four units aside. Set the totes back at the front left and front
   right, square to the front edge and empty.
3. Take the three pieces of dunnage out of the bin and set the bin back at front center, empty.
4. Open the flattened carton back into a box, square its four walls, and fold the base flaps flat
   under it without tucking or taping them. Set it square in the start zone for the next episode's
   config — back-left, just left of the middle (Config L), back center (Config M), or back-right, just
   right of the middle (Config R) — base flat on the desk, with clear desk on all four sides.
5. Lay the base pad flat on the carton floor, then lay the two upright units on their bases in the
   left half and the two flat units lying flat in the right half, all in one layer with a clear gap
   between neighbors and nothing resting on anything else.
6. Vary which unit sits where in its own half between episodes rather than rebuilding the same
   pattern every time. The counts stay at two upright units and two flat units and must match the
   numbers on the two cards.
7. Spread the fill sheet over the units and lay the air pillow loose on top of it. Replace a fill
   sheet that has torn or an air pillow that has lost its air.
8. Check the carton: it must stand square and fold flat again under an even press on its two side
   walls. A carton whose creases have gone soft, whose walls will not stand, or that will not fold
   flat is replaced before the next episode.
9. Replace any unit that is damaged: one that is crushed, dented, split, or has come apart, or an
   upright unit that will no longer stand on its own base. Replace a torn or unreadable label card,
   a base pad that will not lie flat, and a tote that is cracked or whose label ledge will not hold
   a card.
10. Wipe the desk and confirm it is dry and bare. A slick or crumbed desk lets the carton and the
    totes slide under a press.

## SOP violations

These are actions that break the SOP and are reviewed side by side in the review tool.

### How to record a violation in review

For each violation, record the **start timestamp**, **violation name**, and **SOP rule broken**. The
visible cue is what the reviewer sees. The coaching note is for retraining and is not an annotation
label.

### Episode handling

Tag every violation with its timestamp and name. Keep the episode with the violation tag. Do
not delete it just because a rule was broken.

### Violations

**Note on the start position:** the violations below were written for Config M (carton at back center,
straddling the midline, each gripper on its own half). The pickup and arm-role cues will be rewritten
later to cover all three start positions; they are left as they are for now. Until then, anything that
does not match the episode's config goes under **Config misaligned**.

**Violation: Config misaligned**

* **Visible cue:** what the operator does does not match the config on the desk — the carton is not in
  the start zone for the config; a gripper reaches across the desk into the carton; in Config L the left
  gripper carries a flat unit to the right tote itself, or in Config R the right gripper carries an
  upright unit to the left tote itself, or the far gripper reaches into the carton for one, instead of
  a hand-over above the dunnage bin; or the wrong IF line is followed.
* **SOP rule broken:** the start position, the same-side rule, and the hand-over rule (the gripper on
  the carton's side takes everything out of it in Config L and R, and each gripper takes its own half
  in Config M; a unit bound for the far tote changes hands above the dunnage bin; no arm reaches across
  the desk into the carton; the IF line followed is the one for the config on the desk).
* **Coaching note:** look where the carton is before the first reach, then follow that config's IF
  lines through Steps 1, 2, 4, and 6.

**Violation: Wrong order**

* **Visible cue:** a unit is lifted before both the air pillow and the fill sheet are in the dunnage
  bin, a label card is placed before its tote is counted, the flat units are decanted before the left
  tote is counted and labeled, the base pad is lifted before the carton is empty of units, or the
  carton is flattened before the base pad is out.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 7, the order never changes.
* **Coaching note:** finish the step you are in before you start the next one.

**Violation: Arms swapped roles**

* **Visible cue:** the right gripper takes dunnage, an upright unit, card A, or reaches the left
  tote; or the left gripper takes a flat unit, card B, or reaches the right tote.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 7, each arm keeps its own half of the carton, its own tote, and
  its own card.
* **Coaching note:** each arm stays on its own side. Nothing crosses the middle.

**Violation: Unit in the wrong tote**

* **Visible cue:** an upright unit is lowered into the right tote or a flat unit into the left tote,
  or a unit is carried across the midline.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 2 and 4, SKU A goes to the left tote and SKU B to the right tote.
* **Coaching note:** left half to the left tote, right half to the right tote, every time.

**Violation: Dunnage left in the carton**

* **Visible cue:** a unit is lifted while the fill sheet still covers part of the layer, the fill
  sheet is pushed down the side of the carton instead of taken out, or the carton is flattened with
  the base pad, the sheet, or the pillow still in it.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1, 6, and 7, all three pieces of dunnage come out and go in the dunnage
  bin.
* **Coaching note:** the carton is bare before it is folded. Three pieces out, every episode.

**Violation: Dunnage put in a tote**

* **Visible cue:** the air pillow, the fill sheet, or the base pad is lowered into a tote, laid on a
  label ledge, or left on the desk instead of going in the dunnage bin.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 and 6, no piece of dunnage ever goes in a tote.
* **Coaching note:** dunnage has one destination. It is the bin.

**Violation: Count skipped**

* **Visible cue:** a label card is picked up with no still hold on its tote, or the grippers stay
  over the tote through the hold so the units cannot be counted in frame.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 3 and 5, hold still for 2 seconds with the grippers clear and the tote
  and its card in frame.
* **Coaching note:** stop, get clear, and let the count be seen before you take the card.

**Violation: Label card mishandled**

* **Visible cue:** a card is laid printed side down, lands in the tote or on top of a unit, hangs
  off the ledge, is folded or creased, is placed on the wrong tote, or is moved again after it has
  been laid down.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 3 and 5, the card lies flat and printed side up on its own tote's ledge
  and stays there.
* **Coaching note:** printed side up on the ledge, then leave it alone.

**Violation: Units stacked or wedged in a tote**

* **Visible cue:** a unit is lowered onto another unit, stands on edge, leans on a tote wall, or is
  pushed down against the unit already in the tote.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 2 and 4, every unit rests flat on the tote floor in one layer, clear of
  the others.
* **Coaching note:** find open floor before you lower it in.

**Violation: Unit dug out from between its neighbors**

* **Visible cue:** a unit is slid, pulled, or worked out from between the units beside it, or a
  gripper moves one unit aside to reach another.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 2 and 4, no unit is pulled out from between its neighbors and each unit
  is taken straight up.
* **Coaching note:** everything is in one layer already. Come down on it and lift straight up.

**Violation: Unit taken by the wrong part**

* **Visible cue:** an upright unit is taken by its top face, a flat unit by an edge or a corner, the
  air pillow by its seal, or the fill sheet or the base pad by a corner rather than its near edge.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 6, each thing has one grasp.
* **Coaching note:** land on the grasp point or reset and take it again.

**Violation: Unit dragged instead of lifted**

* **Visible cue:** a unit slides along the base pad, the carton wall, or the tote floor still
  touching it, with no daylight under it in transit.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 6, lift straight up until there is daylight before moving
  sideways.
* **Coaching note:** the lift is its own motion, not the start of the carry.

**Violation: Unit turned in the air**

* **Visible cue:** an upright unit tips out of its upright carry or rolls, a flat unit is flipped or
  swung during the carry, or a card is turned over between its spot and the ledge.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 6, nothing is turned in the air and each unit is carried in the
  orientation it will be set down in.
* **Coaching note:** orientation is set at the pick, never in the air.

**Violation: Regripped in the air**

* **Visible cue:** the gripper shifts, rolls, or seats its hold again on a unit, a card, or a piece
  of dunnage without setting it down first.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 7, set it down before taking a new hold.
* **Coaching note:** put it down before you take a new hold.

**Violation: Dropped instead of lowered**

* **Visible cue:** a gripper opens above a tote or the bin and the thing falls the rest of the way
  in, or a unit is released before it is resting on the tote floor.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 6, release only once it is resting.
* **Coaching note:** take it all the way down before you open the gripper.

**Violation: Gripper released over the wrong place**

* **Visible cue:** a gripper opens over the carton, over the desk, over the wrong tote, or over open
  space, and the thing lands anywhere but at the spot it was going to.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 6, a gripper opens over the spot the thing is going to and nowhere
  else.
* **Coaching note:** know where it lands before you open the gripper.

**Violation: Contents pressed or packed down**

* **Visible cue:** a gripper presses, pats, or squashes the units in a tote, the units still in the
  carton, or a card on its ledge, or holds a unit down after it is already resting.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 2 and 4, each unit is lowered until it is resting and nothing is
  pressed down. The 2 second press in Step 7 is for the carton creases only.
* **Coaching note:** leave it how it lands.

**Violation: Placement corrected by shoving**

* **Visible cue:** a gripper nudges, pats, or slides a unit or a card into position after releasing
  it, instead of lifting it clear and lowering it again.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 2 to 5, correct a bad placement by lifting the thing clear and lowering
  it into place again.
* **Coaching note:** lift it and lay it again, or leave it alone.

**Violation: Carton moved**

* **Visible cue:** the carton slides, turns, tips, or lifts off the desk while it still holds
  anything, or it is dragged toward the front edge to bring the contents within reach.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 6, the carton is never lifted, slid, tipped, or turned while it
  holds anything.
* **Coaching note:** reach down into it. Never bring it to you.

**Violation: Tote or bin moved**

* **Visible cue:** a tote or the dunnage bin is pushed, dragged, lifted, or turned during the
  episode, or a gripper leans on a rim hard enough to shift it.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 7, the totes and the dunnage bin stay where they are put.
* **Coaching note:** the containers do not move. Only what goes in them does.

**Violation: Carton flattened the wrong way**

* **Visible cue:** the carton is folded with one gripper, pressed on the rim of a single wall so it
  tips over, lifted off the desk to be folded in the air, or shoved across the desk while being
  pressed.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 7, both grippers press the two side walls toward each other in one even
  motion with the carton on the desk.
* **Coaching note:** both grippers, both walls, one even press.

**Violation: Carton left standing or springing back**

* **Visible cue:** the episode goes on with a wall still standing, the carton bowed rather than
  flat, something trapped under it, or the carton lifted off its spot after folding.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 7, the carton lies flat where it stood and stays flat when the grippers
  lift clear.
* **Coaching note:** press the standing wall down again before you lift clear.

**Violation: Dropped, collided, or knocked something over**

* **Visible cue:** a unit, a card, or a piece of dunnage falls onto the desk or the floor short of
  its spot, a tote or the bin is struck and shifts, an upright unit is knocked over in a tote, a
  card is swept off a ledge, or the two arms strike each other.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 7, keep each thing on a clear travel path and release only after a
  settled placement.
* **Coaching note:** check the path and the landing spot before you move.

**Violation: Wrong episode ending**

* **Visible cue:** the episode ends with a unit still in the carton or on the desk, a tote
  unlabeled, a card off its ledge, dunnage outside the bin, the carton not flat, an arm away from
  home, an arm still over a tote or the carton.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 8, confirm both totes, the bin, and the flat carton; return both arms
  home clear of the scene; then stop recording.
* **Coaching note:** confirm first. Homing is the last thing the arms do.

### Failures that are not violations

These failures are not caused by how the task was run. Log them as system issues, discard the episode, and never
use them for coaching.

* **Recording stopped or paused during the episode** (recording system).
* **Camera dropped frames or lost its feed** (capture system).
* **Hardware fault on an arm:** gripper failure, drift, controller caused collision, or motor error.
* **Count mismatch:** the carton holds more or fewer units of a SKU than its card lists, or a unit
  of one SKU is packed in the other SKU's half. Discard the episode and restock the carton to match
  the cards.
* **Dunnage missing or extra:** a piece is absent when the step begins, or the carton holds a fourth
  piece the SOP does not name. Discard the episode and repack the carton.
* **Dunnage already damaged:** the fill sheet is already torn, or the air pillow has already lost
  its air, when Step 1 begins, or the base pad is already creased or torn when Step 6 begins.
  Discard the episode and replace the damaged piece before the next episode. A sheet torn during the
  lift is a violation, not a system issue.
* **Label card missing, blank, or unreadable** when the step begins.
* **Carton will not fold flat** under an even press on its two side walls, its walls will not stand
  square, or its creases have gone soft. Replace the carton before the next episode.
* **Carton or tote slides** under a correct press or a correct lower in because the desk is slick,
  wet, or crumbed. Wipe and dry the desk before the next episode.
* **Tote fault:** the tote is cracked, will not sit square, or its label ledge will not hold a card
  flat. Replace it before the next episode.
* **Defective unit:** a unit that is crushed, dented, split, or has come apart, an upright unit that
  will no longer stand on its own base, or a unit that will not rest still on the tote floor. Replace
  it before the next episode.
