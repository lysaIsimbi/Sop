# Sort and Bundle Market Produce SOP (1x Episode: 8 Pieces of Produce + 1 Greens Bundle)

One episode weighs and sorts eight pieces of mixed produce into two woven baskets, one basket per
type, then bundles a pile of greens and weighs the bundle. Each type is weighed as a batch of four on
the scale that sits between the two baskets, then moved into its basket, large pieces at the basket
back and small pieces at the basket front.

The order never changes: weigh the onions, fill the onion basket, weigh the potatoes, fill the
potato basket, bundle the greens, then weigh the bundle. Nothing is put in a basket before it has
been weighed as part of its batch of four.

The right gripper takes every piece off the produce tray and loads the scale pan, then fills the potato
basket on its own side. The left gripper fills the onion basket on its own side and carries the greens
onto the twist tie. Both grippers bring a tie end up to cross above the bunch; the left gripper then
holds the bundle while the right gripper twists the tie closed. Neither gripper reaches across into the
other one's basket.

Every piece is carried one at a time and lowered into place until it rests on the floor of the pan or
the basket. Nothing is dropped in from above, nothing is dragged across the table, and no gripper ever
closes on two pieces at once. The scale is never gripped or nudged.

## Setup

Complete both checklists before starting an episode.

### Cell configuration

* **Environment camera:** 900 mm.
* **cell_type:** bimanual

### Hardware checklist

1. Cameras are on and recording.
2. The environment camera shows the whole table: the produce tray at the front right, the cutting
   board at the front center with the twist tie on its right side, and the three back-row things at the
   back center — the onion basket, the scale, and the potato basket.
3. The scale pan and the scale display are both visible in frame, with the whole pan floor in view. The
   display does not have to be readable, but lit and blank must be tellable apart at a glance.
4. Both baskets are visible from above, so the back half and the front half of each one can be seen.
5. The twist tie is visible on the cutting board, so the tie under the stalks and both of its ends can
   be seen.
6. Both arms are at home with grippers open.
7. The table is clean, dry, and bare apart from the produce tray, the two baskets, the scale, the
   cutting board, and the twist tie.
8. The left gripper reaches the scale pan and the onion basket without a long stretch across the table.
   The right gripper reaches the produce tray, the scale pan, the potato basket, the cutting board, and
   the twist tie.

### Materials checklist

1. Eight pieces of **produce** lie loose on the produce tray at the front right, mixed rather than
   grouped: four **onions** and four **potatoes**.
2. Each type has two **large** pieces and two **small** pieces. The difference is obvious. Anyone looking
   at the tray can tell a large onion from a small one without picking either up.
3. Every piece is firm, dry, and clean, and sits still on the tray instead of rolling.
4. Every piece fits inside one gripper in a single grasp.
5. Two empty **woven baskets** stand at the back center, mouths up, one on each side of the scale. The
   **onion basket** is on the left of the scale and the **potato basket** is on the right.
6. Each basket is wide enough to hold four pieces without stacking, and low enough that a piece can be
   lowered in without lifting it over head height.
7. Each basket is heavy enough, and sits firmly enough on the table, to stay put on its own while
   pieces are lowered into it. No gripper ever holds a basket.
8. One **scale** sits at the back center, between the two baskets, switched on, showing zero, with an empty
   pan. Its **on button** is on the top face, clear of the pan, and within the right gripper's reach.
   The scale switches itself off after sitting idle, so it may go to sleep partway through an episode.
9. The scale pan is wide enough to hold four pieces of one type at once without stacking, and the pan
   sits low enough that a piece can be lowered onto it without lifting it over head height.
10. One loose pile of **greens** lies on the **cutting board** at the front center, already gathered into a rough
    bunch, stem ends pointing right toward the twist tie and leaves spread to the left. The board sits far
    enough left that the left gripper reaches the leaf end without crossing the table center.
11. The greens are dry, and the stalks are even enough to gather into one bunch.
12. One **twist tie** lies flat on the right side of the cutting board, clear of the greens, straight
    rather than curled, running from the front edge of the board toward the back.
13. The tie is long enough to wrap once around the gathered stalks and still leave an end sticking out
    each side to twist together, and it holds a twist without springing open.

### Workspace layout

* **Produce tray:** front right, holding the eight mixed pieces.
* **Cutting board:** front center, left of the produce tray, holding the loose greens, stem ends right and leaves
  left. The leaf end sits in the left arm's reach; the stem ends and the twist tie sit in the right arm's reach.
* **Twist tie:** lying flat on the right side of the cutting board, clear of the greens.
* **Scale:** back center, between the two baskets, empty and showing zero.
* **Onion basket:** back center, on the left of the scale, empty.
* **Potato basket:** back center, on the right of the scale, empty.

The scale sits in the middle so both baskets are a short carry from the pan. The three back-row things sit
close enough together that both grippers reach all three.

### Arm assignments

* **Right gripper:** picks every piece off the produce tray, loads the scale pan with both types, moves
  the four **potatoes** off the pan into the **potato basket**, takes the near **tie end** and twists the
  tie closed, lifts the bundle, and works the scale.
* **Left gripper:** moves the four **onions** off the pan into the **onion basket**, lifts the greens onto
  the **twist tie**, takes the far **tie end**, then holds the bundle while the tie is twisted. It never
  touches the produce tray.

Each gripper fills the basket on its own side: onions go left, potatoes go right, so neither gripper
reaches across into the other one's basket. The tie is the one thing both grippers work together: one end
each up to the cross, then the left gripper holds and the right gripper turns.

## Vocabulary

* **Piece:** one onion or one potato. Pieces are picked one at a time and never two in one grasp.
* **Large and small:** the two obvious sizes inside one type. Told apart by eye on the tray, not measured.
* **Type batch:** the eight pieces are two types: four onions and four potatoes. Each type has its own basket,
  and no basket ever holds the other type.
* **Scale pan:** the flat weighing surface of the scale. A **pan load** is what sits on the pan for one weighing.
* **Full pan load:** all four pieces of one type resting on the pan, none stacked, none hanging off the edge, and
  nothing else touching the pan.
* **Free pan count:** the load rests on the pan with no gripper touching it and nothing else on the
  pan, held for a slow count of two.
* **Scale asleep:** the scale display has gone blank because the scale switched itself off while it sat
  idle. Told apart from a lit display at a glance, with no number read.
* **Wake the scale:** the **right gripper** closes to a fist, presses the scale's **on button** straight
  down until the display lights, and lifts straight clear. Nothing else on the scale is touched.
* **Cleared pan:** every piece taken off the pan before the next type is loaded.
* **Basket back:** the half of the basket farthest from the front edge. All large pieces go here.
* **Basket front:** the half of the basket nearest the front edge. All small pieces go here.
* **Settled placement:** the piece is resting on the basket floor or on another piece, is not rolling, and is
  not leaning on the basket wall. The gripper opens only once this is true.
* **Greens:** the loose pile of leafy stalks on the cutting board. The **stem ends** are the cut bottoms
  pointing right. The **leaf end** is the spread leafy side on the left.
* **Twist tie:** a bendable paper or wire tie lying flat on the right side of the cutting board. Its two
  **tie ends** are the parts that stick out each side of the bunch once the greens lie on top of it.
* **Tie closed:** the two tie ends have been turned around each other above the stalks and stay turned
  when the right gripper opens, and the greens hold together as one piece when the left gripper lets go.
  Seen as the tie wrapped all the way round the stalks with the two ends twisted together.
* **Counter-clockwise:** the turning direction seen looking down on the cutting board from above.
* **Bundle:** the greens once the tie is closed on them, held together as one piece that can be lifted by
  the stems.
* **Lift:** coming straight down onto the thing, closing, and raising straight up until there is daylight under it,
  before anything moves sideways.
* **Release point:** over a basket, over the scale pan, over the cutting board, or over the produce tray. A
  gripper opens nowhere else.

## Steps

The order never changes: weigh the onions, fill the onion basket, weigh the potatoes, fill the potato basket,
bundle the greens, weigh the bundle. Each type is weighed as a batch of four before anything ofes in a basket.

### Step 1: Weigh the four onions

**Goal:** the four onions weighed as a batch of four, with nothing else on the pan.

#### 1.1 Load the four onions onto the pan

* The **right gripper** closes on one **onion** on the produce tray, lifts it straight up, carries it level to the
  **scale**, lowers it onto the **pan** until it rests flat, and releases on a **settled placement**.
* Repeat for the other three onions, setting each one on the pan floor rather than on top of another one.
* The **left gripper** stays in frame, clear of the scale.

Take one onion per grasp. Never drop an onion in from above the pan, never roll or drag one across the
table, and never take a potato in this step.

**Check:** four onions rest flat on the scale pan, none stacked, none hanging off the pan edge, no
potato on the pan, and nothing else touching the pan.

#### 1.2 Let the pan weigh the onions

* Leave all four onions free on the pan for a **free pan count**.
* Both grippers stay in frame, clear of the scale.

Nothing touches the pan and nothing else presses down on it during the count.

**Check:** all four onions rested free on the pan for a full count of two with nothing else
touching them.

#### 1.3 Move the onions into the onion basket

* The **left gripper** lifts the two **large onions** off the pan one at a time, carries each one level to the
  **basket back**, and lowers it in until it rests on a **settled placement**.
* The **left gripper** moves the two **small onions** the same way into the **basket front**, one at a time,
  the two of them side by side.
* The **right gripper** stays in frame, clear of the baskets.

Large before small, one onion per grasp. The basket is never held. Never drop an onion in from above the
rim, and never leave one on the pan.

**Check:** four onions sit in the onion basket, both large ones at the basket back and both small ones at the
basket front, none stacked, no onion outside the basket, the scale pan is empty, the scale display is
lit, the basket has not moved, and only potatoes and the greens are left on the produce tray and the
cutting board.

**Retry:** if the display is blank the scale is **asleep**, and the **right gripper** does **wake the
scale** before Step 2 starts.

### Step 2: Weigh the four potatoes

**Goal:** the four potatoes weighed as a batch of four, with nothing else on the pan.

#### 2.1 Load the four potatoes onto the pan

* The **right gripper** carries each **potato** off the produce tray to the **scale**, lowers it onto the **pan**
  until it rests flat, and releases on a **settled placement**.
* All four potatoes sit on the pan, none stacked and nothing hanging off the edge.
* The **left gripper** stays in frame, clear of the scale.

Load the pan only after the produce tray is empty of onions.

#### 2.2 Let the pan weigh the potatoes

* Leave all four potatoes free on the pan for a **free pan count**.
* Both grippers stay in frame, clear of the scale.

**Check:** all four potatoes rested free on the pan for a full count of two with nothing else
touching them.

#### 2.3 Move the potatoes into the potato basket

* The **right gripper** moves the two **large potatoes** into the **basket back**, one at a time, each on a
  **settled placement**.
* The **right gripper** moves the two **small potatoes** into the **basket front**, one at a time.
* The **left gripper** stays in frame, clear of the baskets.

Large before small, one potato per grasp. The basket is never held.

**Check:** four potatoes sit in the potato basket, both large ones at the basket back and both small ones at the
basket front, the produce tray is empty, the scale pan is empty, the baskets have not
moved, and nothing has rolled onto the table.

### Step 3: Tie the greens into a bundle

**Goal:** the twist tie closed around the stalks and the greens held together as one bundle.

#### 3.1 Lay the greens on the tie

* The **left gripper** closes on the greens across the stalks, part way along from the **stem ends**.
* Lift the whole bunch clear of the board and lay it down across the **twist tie**, so the tie crosses
  under the stalks well above the stem ends and a **tie end** sticks out each side of the bunch.
* Release.

Take the whole bunch in one grasp and lay it down in one carry. Lift it clear of the board rather than
dragging it across, so the tie stays where it was laid. Never split the bunch into two grasps.

**Check:** the bunch lies across the twist tie, the tie crosses under the stalks well above the stem
ends, a tie end sticks out each side of the bunch, the tie has not moved, and no stalk has been left
behind on the board.

#### 3.2 Twist the tie closed

* The **left gripper** picks up the far **tie end** and the **right gripper** picks up the near **tie
  end**.
* Both grippers bring their own end straight up over the stalks until the two ends cross above the
  bunch.
* The **left gripper** lets go of its tie end and closes on the **bundle** of greens below the tie,
  holding it down on the cutting board.
* The **right gripper** closes on both crossed ends together and turns them **counter-clockwise**.
* The **right gripper** opens, takes both ends again, and turns counter-clockwise once more. Repeat this
  twist, release, re-grip, twist cycle until the tie is **closed**.
* The **left gripper** lets go of the bundle.

One end per gripper up to the cross, then swap: the left gripper goes onto the greens and the right
gripper does all the turning. Every turn goes counter-clockwise. Never pull the bunch off the board with
a tie end, and never lift the greens while the tie is being turned.

**Check:** the tie is **closed** all the way round the stalks with both ends twisted together, the tie
sits above the stem ends rather than at them, the greens hold together as one **bundle**, no stalk has
been left behind on the cutting board, and the scale display is lit.

**Retry:** if the tie springs open or slips off the stem ends, the **left gripper** lays the greens back
on the cutting board with the stem ends pointing right, and Step 3 runs again from 3.1. If the display is
blank the scale is **asleep**, and the **right gripper** does **wake the scale** before Step 4 starts.

### Step 4: Weigh the bundle

**Goal:** the bundle weighed free on the pan, then back on the cutting board.

* The **right gripper** carries the **bundle** level to the **scale**, lowers it onto the pan until it rests
  flat, and releases.
* Leave the bundle free on the pan for a **free pan count**.
* The **right gripper** lifts the bundle straight up off the pan, carries it to the **cutting board**, lays
  it down with the stem ends pointing right, and releases.

Let the pan carry the whole bundle. Never hold the bundle while it sits on the pan, never let a leaf
hang off the pan onto the table, and never press down on the pan or nudge the scale.

**Check:** the bundle rested fully on the pan for a full count of two with nothing else touching
it, the bundle now lies on the cutting board with the tie still closed, and the scale pan is empty.

### Step 5: Confirm the result and end the episode

* Confirm the onion basket holds four onions with the large ones at the basket back, the potato basket
  holds four potatoes the same way, the produce tray is empty, the bundle lies on the cutting board with
  the tie closed, and the scale pan is empty and showing zero.
* Return both arms home, clear of the baskets, the scale, and the cutting board, then stop recording.

## After the episode: reset the workspace

This reset is not recorded.

1. Take the eight pieces out of the two baskets and lay them back on the produce tray, mixed rather than
   grouped, four onions and four potatoes, two large and two small of each.
2. Wipe both baskets out, check that neither is holding a loose stalk or a piece of skin, and stand them
   side by side at the back center with the scale between them, mouths up.
3. Untwist the tie, take it off the bundle, and throw it away. A tie is used once.
4. Spread the greens back into a loose gathered pile on the cutting board at the front center, stem ends
   pointing right and leaves left.
5. Lay a fresh twist tie flat on the right side of the cutting board, straight and clear of the greens.
6. Clear the scale pan, wipe it dry, and confirm the scale reads zero.
7. Wipe the table and confirm the surface is clean and dry. A wet table lets the baskets and the
   cutting board slide.
8. Replace a piece of produce that is bruised, soft, wet, or split, greens that are wilted or wet, a tie
   that is bent, split, or will not hold a twist, and a basket that is split, misshapen, or rocking.

## SOP violations

These are actions that break the SOP and are reviewed side by side in the review tool.

### How to record a violation in review

For each violation, record the **start timestamp**, **violation name**, and **SOP rule broken**. The
visible cue is what the reviewer sees. The coaching note is for retraining and is not an annotation
label.

### Episode handling

Tag every violation with its timestamp and name. Keep the episode with the violation tagged. Do not delete
it just because a rule was broken.

### Violations

**Violation: Wrong order**

* **Visible cue:** a potato goes onto the scale before all four onions are in the onion basket, the greens
  are bundled before the produce tray is empty, or the bundle goes on the scale before the tie is closed.
  Inside a basket, a small piece goes in before both large ones.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 5, weigh onions, then potatoes, then the greens, then the
  bundle weigh, with large pieces placed before small ones in each basket.
* **Coaching note:** clear the tray before you touch the greens, and always place large before small.

**Violation: Arms swapped roles**

* **Visible cue:** the left gripper takes a piece off the produce tray, loads the pan, puts a potato in a
  basket, lifts the bundle, or works the scale, or the right gripper puts an onion in a basket or carries
  the greens onto the tie.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 4, the right gripper picks, loads the pan, fills the potato basket,
  lifts the bundle, and weighs; the left gripper fills the onion basket and carries the greens; both work
  the tie, one end each up to the cross, then the left holds the bundle and the right turns.
* **Coaching note:** each gripper fills the basket on its own side. Onions left, potatoes right.

**Violation: Weighing skipped**

* **Visible cue:** a piece goes from the produce tray into a basket with no weighing on the scale in between,
  or the bundle goes on the cutting board with no weighing.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 4, every type goes on the scale before it goes in a basket, and
  the bundle is weighed before it is laid back down.
* **Coaching note:** nothing reaches a basket or the board until it has been weighed.

**Violation: Wrong load on the pan**

* **Visible cue:** the count is done with fewer or more than the four pieces of one type on the pan,
  both types sit on the pan at once, or a piece is left on the produce tray while the count is done.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1.1 and 2.1, all four pieces of one type sit on the pan for the
  count, and nothing else.
* **Coaching note:** count the four on the pan before you let go.

**Violation: Load not free on the pan during its count**

* **Visible cue:** a gripper is still closed on the piece or the bundle during the count, or a
  gripper is resting on the pan or holding the pan down.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1.2, 2.2, and 4, the load rests free on the pan for the count.
* **Coaching note:** let go, then count.

**Violation: Pan not cleared before the next weighing**

* **Visible cue:** an onion is still on the pan when the potatoes are loaded onto it, a piece is still on
  the pan when the bundle is put on.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1.3, 2.3, and 4, the pan is cleared before the next thing goes on.
* **Coaching note:** clear the pan before the next load goes on.

**Violation: Piece in the wrong basket or left out**

* **Visible cue:** at the end of Step 2 an onion sits in the potato basket, a potato sits in the onion
  basket, or a piece is left on the produce tray, on the scale pan, on the table, or on the floor.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1.3 and 2.3, all four onions into the onion basket and all four potatoes
  into the potato basket.
* **Coaching note:** name the type before you place it. The onion basket is the left one.

**Violation: Large and small not split back to front**

* **Visible cue:** at the end of Step 1 or Step 2 a large piece sits in the basket front, a small piece
  sits in the basket back, the sizes are mixed through the basket, or a piece is stacked on another
  instead of beside it.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1.3 and 2.3, large pieces into the basket back and small pieces into the
  basket front, none stacked.
* **Coaching note:** back half is for the big ones. Fill it first and the front stays clear for the small
  ones.

**Violation: Piece dragged or doubled up**

* **Visible cue:** a piece is rolled or dragged across the table instead of lifted and carried, two
  pieces are taken in one grasp, or the gripper releases while the piece is still rolling or leaning on
  the basket wall.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1.1, 1.3, 2.1, and 2.3, one piece per grasp, lifted and carried level,
  lowered until it rests, and released only on a settled placement.
* **Coaching note:** take it down to the floor, wait until it sits still, then open.

**Violation: Basket held**

* **Visible cue:** a gripper closes on a basket rim or steadies a basket while a piece goes in.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1.3 and 2.3, pieces go into a basket that stands on its own, and no gripper
  holds a basket.
* **Coaching note:** hands off the basket. If it shifts when a piece goes in, report it rather than
  holding it.

**Violation: Piece set down mid-route**

* **Visible cue:** a piece is set down on the table, laid back on the produce tray after being picked up,
  laid back on the pan after its count, or parked anywhere between the produce tray, the scale pan, and
  the basket instead of going straight through.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1.1, 1.3, 2.1, and 2.3, each piece goes tray to pan and pan to basket in
  one carry, and the pan is cleared before the next type is loaded.
* **Coaching note:** one pick, one carry, one place. Once a piece is off the pan, it stays off.

**Violation: Scale not woken, or woken wrong**

* **Visible cue:** pieces or the bundle go on the pan while the display is still blank, the wake happens
  after the load is already on the pan, a gripper presses the pan or the body of the scale instead of the
  on button, the fist stays resting on the scale after the display lights, the scale shifts or turns under
  the press, or the left gripper does the pressing.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1.3 and 3.2, the check confirms the display is lit before the next weighing,
  and a blank display is fixed by the right gripper pressing the on button straight down and lifting
  straight clear, before anything goes on the pan.
* **Coaching note:** lit display first, then load. Button only, straight down, straight off.

**Violation: Moved on without the step's check**

* **Visible cue:** the next step starts before the check at the end of the current one is done, for example the
  onions still on the produce tray when the potato weighing starts, or a piece still on the scale pan when
  the greens are being bundled.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 4, each step ends with its check before the next one starts.
* **Coaching note:** look before you move on.

**Violation: Greens not laid square on the tie**

* **Visible cue:** the greens land beside the twist tie rather than across it, the tie ends up at the
  stem ends instead of well above them, the tie is dragged out of place by the greens, or the bunch is
  laid down in two grasps.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 3.1, the left gripper lays the whole bunch down in one carry with the tie
  crossing under the stalks well above the stem ends and an end sticking out each side.
* **Coaching note:** look at where the tie is before you let go, not after.

**Violation: Greens dragged instead of lifted onto the tie**

* **Visible cue:** the left gripper pushes or slides the bunch across the board onto the twist tie
  instead of lifting it clear, or the bunch scuffs the tie and shifts it on the way down.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 3.1, the bunch is lifted clear of the board and laid down on the tie.
* **Coaching note:** pick it up, carry it over, set it down. Do not push it along.

**Violation: Tie twisted wrong**

* **Visible cue:** one gripper takes both tie ends before they cross above the bunch, the left gripper
  stays on its tie end instead of moving onto the greens once they cross, the left gripper is off the
  bundle while the right gripper turns, or a turn goes clockwise.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 3.2, one end per gripper up to the cross, then the left gripper holds the
  bundle while the right gripper turns the crossed ends counter-clockwise.
* **Coaching note:** one end each on the way up, then swap: left onto the greens, right onto the twist.
  Always counter-clockwise.

**Violation: Tie not closed**

* **Visible cue:** at the end of Step 3 the tie ends are crossed but not turned around each other, the
  tie springs open when the grippers let go, the tie sits down at the stem ends instead of above
  them, the tie has caught the leaves rather than the stalks, or the greens come away loose with no tie
  on them.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 3.2, both tie ends come up over the stalks and are turned around each other
  until the tie stays turned when both grippers open.
* **Coaching note:** turn it, let go, and watch. If it springs open, take the ends and turn again.

**Violation: Stalks left out of the tie**

* **Visible cue:** the tie closes on part of the bunch and one or more stalks are left loose on the
  cutting board or hanging free of the tie.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 3.1, the left gripper takes the whole bunch in one grasp and lays all of it
  across the tie.
* **Coaching note:** take the whole bunch in one grasp. Check the board is bare before you tie.

**Violation: Bundle set down in the wrong place**

* **Visible cue:** after the weighing the bundle is laid on the table, on the produce tray, back on the
  or left on the scale pan instead of on the cutting board.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 4, the bundle goes back on the cutting board with the stem ends pointing
  right.
* **Coaching note:** the board is where the bundle lives.

**Violation: Collided or knocked something over**

* **Visible cue:** the produce tray, a basket, the scale, or the cutting board is struck and shifts, or
  the two arms strike each other.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 4, each thing is carried on a travel path that stays clear of the
  produce tray, the baskets, the scale, the cutting board, and the other arm.
* **Coaching note:** check the path before you move.

**Violation: Wrong episode ending**

* **Visible cue:** the episode ends with a piece still on the produce tray, a piece in the wrong basket,
  the greens loose or untied, the bundle still on the scale pan, a basket or the scale
  out of place, an arm away from home, or an arm still over a basket or the scale.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 5, confirm the two baskets, the empty tray, the bundle on the cutting board,
  and the empty pan; return both arms home clear of the scene; then stop recording.
* **Coaching note:** confirm first. Homing is the last thing the arms do.

### Failures that are not violations

These failures are not caused by how the task was run. Log them as system issues, discard the episode,
and never use them for coaching.

* **Recording stopped or paused during the episode** (recording system).
* **Camera dropped frames or lost its feed** (capture system).
* **Hardware fault on an arm:** gripper failure, drift, controller-caused collision, or motor error.
* **Defective produce:** a piece that is soft, split, or wet enough to slip out of a correct grasp, or one
  that will not sit still on the tray. Replace it before the next episode.
* **Defective greens:** wilted or wet stalks that will not hold together in one grasp, or a bunch so
  loose it cannot be lifted clear of the board in one carry. Replace them before the next episode.
* **Tie fault:** the twist tie snaps while it is being turned, springs open every time however well it is
  turned, or is too short to reach round the stalks. Replace it before the next episode.
* **Defective basket:** split, misshapen, rocking on the table, or so light that it slides when a piece
  is lowered into it. Replace it before the next episode.
* **Scale fault:** the pan does not sit level, or the scale will not return to zero at setup.
  Replace or rezero it before the next episode.
* **A basket or the cutting board slides** under a correct hold because the table is wet
  or slick. Wipe and dry the table before the next episode.

## Annotation subtasks (from SOP)

1. Load the four onions onto the scale pan
2. Let the pan weigh the onions
3. Move the two large onions into the basket back with the left gripper
4. Move the two small onions into the basket front with the left gripper
5. Load the four potatoes onto the scale pan
6. Let the pan weigh the potatoes
7. Move the two large potatoes into the basket back with the right gripper
8. Move the two small potatoes into the basket front with the right gripper
9. Lift the greens onto the twist tie with the left gripper
10. Bring both tie ends up to cross above the bunch
11. Hold the bundle and twist the tie closed
12. Weigh the bundle on the scale
13. Lay the bundle back on the cutting board
14. Confirm the result and end the episode
