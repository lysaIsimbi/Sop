# Care for Three Houseplants SOP (1x Episode: 3 plants)

**One episode cares for all three houseplants.** The episode runs from three potted plants standing
in a short row, each with two dead leaves on it, a dry pot mark facing the front table edge and a
saucer with old drips on it, to three plants with no dead leaves, each pot turned a half turn, each
pot watered to its own spec, and each saucer wiped dry.

The plants are worked **one at a time, in order: plant 1, then plant 2, then plant 3.** Nothing on
the next plant is touched until the plant before it passes its check.

Five jobs are done on each plant, and the order never changes: **prune the dead leaves, clear the
trimmings into the bin, rotate the pot, water the pot to spec, wipe the saucer.** Dry work comes
first and wet work comes last, so nothing is handled after the water goes in.

The two grippers have fixed jobs and never swap. The **right gripper is the primary gripper**: it
works the snips, the pot itself, and the watering can. The **left gripper is the support gripper**:
it holds the saucer still while the pot turns, moves cut leaves to the bin, and wipes the saucer with
the cloth. The right gripper never touches a leaf on the saucer, the bin, or the cloth. The left
gripper never touches the snips, the pot, or the can.

Only one tool is out of its spot at a time. Every tool goes back to its own spot before the next one
is picked up. Nothing is passed from one gripper to the other.

Never lift a pot off its saucer. Never turn a plant, a leaf, or the can in the air. Never move a
saucer out of the row. Never carry the can tipped. Never wipe anything but a saucer.

## Setup

Complete both checklists before starting an episode.

### Cell configuration

* **Environment camera:** 900 mm.
* **cell_type:** bimanual

### Hardware checklist

1. Cameras are on and recording.
2. The environment camera is centered on the table and its frame includes all three saucers, all
   three pots from soil to top leaf, the bin, the cloth spot, the snips spot, the can spot, and the
   front table edge.
3. Both arms are at home with grippers open.
4. The table holds nothing but the three plants on their saucers, the bin, the cloth, the snips, and
   the watering can.
5. The right arm reaches all three pots, the snips spot, and the can spot from the front without
   extending to a joint limit.
6. The left arm reaches all three saucers, the bin, and the cloth spot the same way.
7. Move the three saucers closer together until both of those are true. The row must stay short
   enough that neither arm stretches to reach the far plant.
8. Arm paths between the plant row and the front strip are clear.

### Materials checklist

1. Three potted houseplants, each standing in the middle of its own saucer, in one short row across
   the middle of the table.
2. **Plant 1** is on the left and stands in the small pot. **Plant 2** is in the middle and stands in
   the large pot. **Plant 3** is on the right and stands in the medium pot.
3. Each pot has a **pot mark** on its outside, and every mark faces the front table edge at episode
   start.
4. Each plant carries exactly **two dead leaves**: brown, dry, and easy to tell apart from the green
   leaves at a glance.
5. Every pot is dry on the outside, and its soil is dry on top.
6. Every saucer carries old drips, so there is something visible to wipe. No saucer holds standing
   water.
7. The **water spec** for each plant is fixed by its place in the row:

   | Plant | Place in the row | Pot | Water spec |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | Plant 1 | left | small | 2 seconds |
   | Plant 2 | middle | large | 4 seconds |
   | Plant 3 | right | medium | 3 seconds |

8. The watering can stands upright on the can spot. It has a handle the right gripper can close on
   and a narrow spout. It is light enough for one gripper to lift and holds enough water for all
   three pours with water to spare.
9. The snips stand on the snips spot with their blades open and pointing away from the front table
   edge.
10. The cloth lies folded flat on the cloth spot. It is dry and clean.
11. The bin stands open on the bin spot. It is empty, low, wide at the mouth, and heavy enough that
    it does not slide when a leaf drops into it.

### Workspace layout

Nothing on the table is marked or taped out. Every area below is a piece of the bare tabletop, judged
by eye against the table edges and the plants themselves.

* **Front table edge:** the table edge nearest the station. Every direction in this task is judged
  against it.
* **Plant row:** the short line across the middle of the table where the three saucers stand. Plant 1
  on the left, plant 2 in the middle, plant 3 on the right. Saucers do not move during the episode.
* **Front strip:** the clear strip of table between the plant row and the front table edge. It holds
  the four tool spots.
* **Snips spot:** on the front strip, right of the middle. Home of the snips.
* **Can spot:** on the front strip, right of the middle, beside the snips spot. Home of the watering
  can.
* **Cloth spot:** on the front strip, left of the middle. Home of the cloth.
* **Bin spot:** on the front strip, left of the middle, beside the cloth spot. Home of the bin.
* **Arm home:** the resting pose for each arm, gripper open and clear of the table.

### Arm assignments

* **Right gripper (primary):** does the work that decides the take. It picks up and uses the snips,
  it turns the pot, and it picks up, pours, and returns the watering can. It never touches a cut
  leaf, the bin, or the cloth.
* **Left gripper (support):** holds the saucer still while the right gripper turns the pot, picks cut
  leaves off the saucer and drops them in the bin, and wipes the saucer with the cloth. It never
  touches the snips, the pot, or the can.

## Vocabulary

* **Plant 1, plant 2, plant 3:** the three plants named by their place in the row, left to right.
  Always worked in that order.
* **Pot:** the container the plant grows in. It stands on its saucer and is never lifted off it.
* **Saucer:** the shallow dish under the pot. It catches drips and stays where it stands.
* **Pot mark:** the small mark on the outside of a pot. It faces the front table edge before the
  turn and faces away from the front table edge after it.
* **Dead leaf:** a brown, dry, limp leaf. Each plant has exactly two. A green leaf is never a dead
  leaf.
* **Snips:** spring-loaded cutters. The blades sit open on their own. The right gripper holding them
  lightly keeps the blades open, and the right gripper closing all the way squeezes them shut and
  makes the cut.
* **Cut at the stem:** the open blades sit around the stem of the dead leaf, close to where that stem
  meets the plant, and the cut is made there. Never cut across the middle of a leaf.
* **Quarter turn:** the right gripper closes on the pot, turns it about a quarter of the way around,
  and opens. Two quarter turns in a row make a half turn.
* **Half turn:** the pot has turned far enough that the pot mark, which faced the front table edge,
  now faces away from it.
* **Water spec:** the number of seconds the can is held tipped over that plant's pot: 2 seconds at
  plant 1, 4 seconds at plant 2, 3 seconds at plant 3.
* **Pour position:** the spout is over the soil, inside the pot rim, held low and clear of the
  leaves.
* **Level carry:** the can is held upright the whole way to the plant and the whole way back. Water
  never runs while the can is moving.
* **Wipe lap:** the cloth is pressed on the saucer beside the pot and run once all the way around the
  pot, touching the saucer the whole way.
* **Saucer dry:** no drip, puddle, or wet ring is left on the saucer.
* **Saucer clear:** no cut leaf, no soil, and nothing else lies on the saucer.
* **Steady hold:** the left gripper closes lightly on the saucer rim, holds the saucer flat on the
  table without lifting or sliding it, and does not open until the step says so.
* **Idle arm:** the arm not named in a step. An idle arm waits at home, clear of the table. It never
  steadies a pot, catches a leaf, or helps the working arm.
* **Tool spot:** the snips spot, the can spot, the cloth spot, or the bin spot. A tool is out of its
  spot only while it is being used, and only one tool is out at a time.
* **Stable placement:** a tool stays still after the gripper opens, sits fully on its own spot, and
  does not rock, lean, or slide.
* **Release point:** a gripper opens only over a tool spot, over the bin mouth, or over the saucer it
  is working. Never over soil, never over a plant, never over bare table.

## Steps

Steps 1 to 5 are done on one plant, then repeated on the next. Work plant 1 first, then plant 2, then
plant 3. Step 6 decides whether another plant is left. Step 7 ends the episode.

### Step 1: Prune the two dead leaves

**Goal:** both dead leaves are cut off the plant and lying on its saucer.

* The **right gripper** closes on the snips handles at the snips spot and lifts them straight up.
  Hold the handles lightly so the blades stay open.
* Carry the snips to the first dead leaf and put the open blades around its stem, close to where that
  stem meets the plant.
* Close the **right gripper** all the way to make the cut. The leaf falls onto the saucer.
* Open the **right gripper** part way so the blades spring open again, then move to the second dead
  leaf and cut it the same way.
* Carry the snips back and stand them on the snips spot with the blades open and pointing away from
  the front table edge, then open the **right gripper** and lift clear.
* Never cut in the air, never cut across the middle of a leaf, and never drag the snips over the
  soil.

The left arm is the idle arm for the whole of Step 1.

**Check:** no brown leaf is left on the plant, and no green leaf has been cut. If a dead leaf is
still attached, cut it again with the **right gripper**. Two extra cuts at most on the plant. If it
still will not cut, put the snips back on the snips spot, end the episode, and check the snips in the
reset.

**Expected state:** the plant carries only green leaves, two cut leaves lie on the saucer, and the
snips stand on the snips spot.

### Step 2: Clear the trimmings into the bin

**Goal:** both cut leaves are in the bin and the saucer is clear.

One leaf per trip.

* The **left gripper** closes on one cut leaf where it lies and lifts it straight up.
* Carry it level to the bin, hold it inside the bin mouth, then open the gripper and let it drop.
* Pick up the second leaf the same way and drop it in the bin.
* If a cut leaf landed on the table instead of the saucer, the **left gripper** picks it up from
  there the same way.
* Never sweep or push a leaf off the saucer, never open the gripper outside the bin mouth, and never
  carry both leaves at once.

The right arm is the idle arm for the whole of Step 2.

**Check:** the saucer is clear, no leaf lies on the table around the plant, and both cut leaves are
in the bin. If a leaf is still out, the **left gripper** picks it up and drops it in the bin. Two
extra tries at most on the plant.

### Step 3: Rotate the pot a half turn

**Goal:** the pot mark faces away from the front table edge, and the saucer has not moved.

Two quarter turns, one after the other.

* The **left gripper** closes lightly on the saucer rim and holds the saucer flat on the table. This
  steady hold lasts until the end of the step.
* The **right gripper** closes on the pot body, below the rim, firm enough to turn the pot but not to
  lift it.
* Turn the pot a quarter turn, keeping the pot standing on the saucer the whole way.
* Open the **right gripper**, lift clear, close on the pot body again, and turn a second quarter
  turn the same way.
* Open the **right gripper** and lift clear, then the **left gripper** opens and lifts clear.
* Never lift the pot off its saucer, and never turn the pot in one long twist.

**Check:** the pot mark faces away from the front table edge, the pot still stands in the middle of
its saucer, and the saucer sits where it started. If the mark is still toward the front, give the pot
one more quarter turn with the **right gripper** while the **left gripper** holds the saucer. If the
pot has crept off the middle of the saucer, the **right gripper** nudges it back to the middle once.

**Expected state:** the pot mark faces away from the front table edge and the plant stands as it did
before, only turned.

### Step 4: Water the pot to spec

**Goal:** the plant has been given its water spec and the can stands back on the can spot.

* The **right gripper** closes on the can handle at the can spot and lifts the can straight up.
* Carry the can level to the plant. Never tip it on the way.
* Hold the spout in the pour position: over the soil, inside the pot rim, low and clear of the
  leaves.
* Tip the can until water runs from the spout, and hold it there for this plant's water spec: **2
  seconds at plant 1, 4 seconds at plant 2, 3 seconds at plant 3.**
* Bring the can back upright over the pot and wait until the spout stops dripping.
* Carry the can level back to the can spot and stand it down. Open the **right gripper** only once
  the can stands on its own.
* Never pour on the leaves, never pour outside the pot rim, and never move the can while water is
  running.

The left arm is the idle arm for the whole of Step 4.

**Check:** the top of the soil is wet, no water has gone outside the saucer, and the can stands
upright on the can spot. If no water ran at all, pour again once with the same count. If water went
outside the saucer, end the episode and mop up in the reset.

**Expected state:** wet soil, drips on the saucer, the can upright on its spot.

### Step 5: Wipe the saucer

**Goal:** the saucer is dry and the cloth lies back on the cloth spot.

* The **left gripper** closes on the cloth at the cloth spot and lifts it straight up.
* Press the cloth down on the saucer beside the pot and run one wipe lap all the way around the pot,
  keeping the cloth touching the saucer the whole way.
* Keep the cloth on the saucer. Never wipe the soil, the pot, the plant, or the table.
* Carry the cloth back, lay it flat on the cloth spot, then open the gripper and lift clear.

The right arm is the idle arm for the whole of Step 5.

**Check:** the saucer is dry, the pot still stands in the middle of its saucer, and the cloth lies
flat on the cloth spot. If a drip is left, the **left gripper** takes the cloth again and runs one
more wipe lap. One extra lap at most on the plant.

### Step 6: Finish the plant and take the next one

**Goal:** the finished plant is confirmed, and the next plant is started or the episode moves to its
ending.

* Confirm the plant just worked: no brown leaf on it, no green leaf cut, the saucer clear, the pot
  mark facing away from the front table edge, the soil wet, the saucer dry.
* Confirm the tools: the snips on the snips spot with their blades open, the can upright on the can
  spot, the cloth flat on the cloth spot.
* **If a plant is still unworked**, go back to Step 1 and work the next plant in order: plant 1, then
  plant 2, then plant 3.
* **If all three plants are done**, go on to Step 7.
* Never start the next plant before the plant before it passes this check.

### Step 7: Confirm the row and end the episode

* Confirm all three plants: every dead leaf cut off, every saucer clear, every pot mark facing away
  from the front table edge, every soil wet, every saucer dry.
* Confirm the row: all three pots standing in the middle of their own saucers, all three saucers
  where they started.
* Confirm the bin: six cut leaves in it and nothing on the table around it.
* Confirm the tools: the snips on the snips spot, the can upright on the can spot, the cloth flat on
  the cloth spot.
* Fix a failed check only with the fix written in its step. If no fix is written, or the fix would
  mean going back through the order, end the episode and reset instead.
* Return both arms home with grippers open, then stop recording.

## After the episode: reset the workspace

This reset is not recorded.

1. Empty the bin and stand it back open and empty on the bin spot.
2. Dry every saucer and wipe up any water that went outside a saucer.
3. Turn each pot back until its pot mark faces the front table edge, and stand each pot in the middle
   of its own saucer.
4. Put two fresh dead leaves on each plant, brown and dry and easy to tell from the green leaves.
5. Put old drips back on each saucer so there is something to wipe, with no standing water.
6. Dry the outside of each pot and let the soil dry on top before the next episode. Swap in a plant
   with dry soil if the last one is still wet.
7. Refill the watering can and stand it upright on the can spot.
8. Stand the snips on the snips spot with the blades open and pointing away from the front table
   edge, and lay a dry folded cloth flat on the cloth spot.
9. Replace a plant that has lost leaves it should not have lost, a pot that will not stand flat in
   its saucer, a saucer that rocks, snips that will not cut, and a can that drips on the carry.
10. Run both Setup checklists again.

## SOP violations

These are actions that break the SOP and are reviewed side by side in the review tool.

### How to record a violation in review

For each violation, record the **start timestamp**, **violation name**, and **SOP rule broken**. The
visible cue is what the reviewer sees. The coaching note is for retraining and is not an annotation
label.

### Episode handling

Tag every violation with its timestamp and name. Keep the episode with the violation tag. Do not
delete it just because a rule was broken.

### Violations

**Violation: Plants worked out of order**

* **Visible cue:** a gripper starts work on plant 2 or plant 3 before the plant before it is
  finished, or work goes back to a plant that was already finished.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 6, plants are worked one at a time in the order plant 1, plant 2,
  plant 3.
* **Coaching note:** finish one plant end to end, confirm it, then take the next.

**Violation: Jobs done out of order on a plant**

* **Visible cue:** on one plant, the pot is watered before it is turned, the pot is turned before the
  cut leaves are cleared, the saucer is wiped before the pour, or a leaf is cut after the pour.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 5, on every plant the order is prune, clear the trimmings, rotate,
  water, wipe.
* **Coaching note:** dry work first, wet work last. Nothing is handled after the water goes in.

**Violation: Snips gripped or used wrong**

* **Visible cue:** the right gripper squeezes the snips shut while carrying them, closes the blades
  away from a stem, cuts across the middle of a leaf, or drags the snips over the soil or the saucer.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 1, the right gripper holds the snips lightly so the blades stay open and
  closes fully only with the blades around the stem of a dead leaf.
* **Coaching note:** the grip is the cut. Hold light on the way in, close only when the stem is
  between the blades.

**Violation: Dead leaf left on the plant**

* **Visible cue:** a brown, dry leaf is still attached when the right gripper puts the snips back, or
  a plant still shows a brown leaf at the end of the episode.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 1, both dead leaves are cut off the plant before the snips go back on the
  snips spot.
* **Coaching note:** two dead leaves on every plant. Look all the way round the plant before the
  snips go back.

**Violation: Green leaf cut**

* **Visible cue:** the right gripper cuts a green leaf, or a green leaf falls onto the saucer or the
  table after a cut.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 1, only brown, dry leaves are cut, at the stem.
* **Coaching note:** read the colour of the leaf before the blades go round it. A green leaf is never
  a dead leaf.

**Violation: Cut leaf not put in the bin**

* **Visible cue:** a cut leaf is still on the saucer or the table when the plant is finished, the
  left gripper sweeps or pushes a leaf instead of picking it up, the left gripper opens outside the
  bin mouth, or it carries both leaves in one trip.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 2, the left gripper picks up one cut leaf per trip and drops it inside
  the bin mouth.
* **Coaching note:** one leaf, one trip, released inside the bin. A swept leaf lands anywhere.

**Violation: Saucer not held during the turn**

* **Visible cue:** the right gripper turns the pot while the left gripper is at home or off the
  saucer, or the left gripper lets go before the right gripper has finished both quarter turns.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 3, the left gripper's steady hold on the saucer rim lasts until the end
  of the step.
* **Coaching note:** hold first, turn second, let go last. An unheld saucer turns with the pot.

**Violation: Pot lifted off its saucer**

* **Visible cue:** the right gripper picks the pot up, tilts it, or slides it clear of the saucer at
  any point.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 3, the pot stays standing on its saucer the whole way through the turn.
* **Coaching note:** grip firm enough to turn, never firm enough to lift.

**Violation: Pot not turned a half turn**

* **Visible cue:** the pot mark still faces the front table edge after the turn, or the right gripper
  makes one long twist instead of two quarter turns.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 3, two quarter turns, and the pot mark ends facing away from the front
  table edge.
* **Coaching note:** two quarter turns, regripping between them. Read the mark before you move on.

**Violation: Pot or saucer left out of place**

* **Visible cue:** the pot ends off the middle of its saucer, a saucer ends moved out of the row, or
  a saucer slides during a turn or a wipe.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 3 and 5, the pot ends in the middle of its saucer and the saucer sits
  where it started.
* **Coaching note:** the saucer is furniture. Nothing about this task moves it.

**Violation: Can tipped or dripping on the carry**

* **Visible cue:** the right gripper carries the can leaning, water runs from the spout while the can
  is moving, or the can is carried away before the spout stops dripping.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 4, the can is carried level, and after the pour it comes upright over the
  pot and waits until the spout stops dripping.
* **Coaching note:** upright on the way in, upright on the way out, and never move while it runs.

**Violation: Pour does not match the water spec**

* **Visible cue:** the tipped hold is plainly shorter or longer than this plant's count, or the
  count used belongs to a different plant, or the pour is broken into several tips.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 4, hold the tip for 2 seconds at plant 1, 4 seconds at plant 2, and 3
  seconds at plant 3, in one pour.
* **Coaching note:** know the count before you lift the can. One tip, one count, per plant.

**Violation: Water put where it does not belong**

* **Visible cue:** the spout sits outside the pot rim, over the leaves, or high above the pot, or
  water lands on a leaf, on the outside of a pot, on a saucer instead of the soil, or on the table.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 4, the spout is held over the soil, inside the pot rim, low and clear of
  the leaves.
* **Coaching note:** put the spout inside the rim and low before you tip. A high pour goes
  everywhere.

**Violation: Can released before it stands**

* **Visible cue:** the right gripper opens while the can is still in the air, still leaning, or not
  fully on the can spot, or the can rocks or falls after release.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 4, the right gripper opens only once the can stands on its own on the can
  spot.
* **Coaching note:** feel it take its own weight, then open.

**Violation: Wipe not done as written**

* **Visible cue:** the left gripper wipes part of the way around the pot, lifts the cloth off the
  saucer mid lap, dabs instead of wiping, or wipes the soil, the pot, the plant, or the table.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 5, one wipe lap all the way around the pot, cloth touching the saucer the
  whole way, and nothing but a saucer is wiped.
* **Coaching note:** press down, go all the way round in one lap, stay on the saucer.

**Violation: Saucer left wet**

* **Visible cue:** a drip, puddle, or wet ring is still on the saucer when the left gripper puts the
  cloth back, or at the end of the episode.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 5, the saucer is dry before the cloth goes back on the cloth spot.
* **Coaching note:** look at the whole saucer, not just the side you wiped last.

**Violation: Tool left off its spot, or two tools out at once**

* **Visible cue:** the snips, the can, or the cloth ends anywhere but its own spot, is left lying on
  the plant row or the table, is put back leaning or rocking, or one tool is picked up before the one
  before it is back on its spot.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1, 4, and 5, each tool goes back on its own spot before the next tool is
  picked up.
* **Coaching note:** one tool out at a time, and it goes home before the next one comes out.

**Violation: Wrong gripper used**

* **Visible cue:** the left gripper touches the snips, a pot, or the can; or the right gripper
  touches a cut leaf, the bin, or the cloth.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 5, the right gripper works the snips, the pot, and the can, and the
  left gripper works the leaves, the bin, and the cloth.
* **Coaching note:** each gripper has its own three jobs. Nothing is handed over.

**Violation: Idle arm gets in the way**

* **Visible cue:** the arm not named in a step comes in, steadies a pot, catches a falling leaf,
  holds the can, or helps the working arm, or both arms work at the same time outside the steady hold
  in Step 3.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1, 2, 4, and 5, the idle arm waits at home clear of the table.
* **Coaching note:** one arm works, the other waits at home. The only two-arm moment is the turn.

**Violation: More than one thing handled in one trip**

* **Visible cue:** a gripper carries two leaves, two tools, or a tool and a leaf together, or holds a
  tool while doing another job.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 5, one leaf per trip and one tool at a time.
* **Coaching note:** one thing in the gripper, every trip.

**Violation: Retry limit exceeded**

* **Visible cue:** more than two extra cuts on a plant, more than two extra tries to pick up a leaf,
  more than one extra pour, or more than one extra wipe lap.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1, 2, 4, and 5, each retry limit as written in that step's check.
* **Coaching note:** retry the number of times the step allows, then end the episode and fix it in
  the reset.

**Violation: Plant check skipped**

* **Visible cue:** a gripper moves on to the next plant, or the episode ends, without the finished
  plant and the tool spots being looked over.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 6, confirm the plant and the three tool spots before taking the next
  plant.
* **Coaching note:** the check is part of the plant. Look before you move on.

**Violation: Something dropped, knocked over, or spilled**

* **Visible cue:** a leaf is dropped outside the bin, a pot or plant is knocked over, a saucer is
  tipped, the can or the bin is knocked over or spills, an arm hits a plant on the way in or out, or
  soil comes out of a pot.
* **SOP rule broken:** Steps 1 to 5, move in and out clear, and open a gripper only over a tool spot,
  the bin mouth, or the saucer being worked.
* **Coaching note:** check the path and the landing spot before you move. Come in from the front and
  above.

**Violation: Wrong episode ending**

* **Visible cue:** the episode ends with a failed check not fixed, a dead leaf still on a plant, a
  leaf on a saucer or the table, a pot mark still facing the front table edge, a dry pot, a wet
  saucer, a tool off its spot, an arm near home but not at it, or a gripper not fully open.
* **SOP rule broken:** Step 7, confirm all three plants, the row, the bin, and the tools, then return
  both arms home with grippers open, then stop recording.
* **Coaching note:** confirm first. Homing is the last thing the arms do.

### Non-violation failures

These failures are not caused by how the task was run. Log them as system issues, discard the
episode, and never use them for coaching.

* **Recording stopped or paused during the episode** (recording system).
* **Camera dropped frames or lost its feed** (capture system).
* **Hardware fault on an arm:** gripper failure, drift, controller caused collision, or motor error.
* **Faulty snips:** blades that will not spring open, or that crush a stem instead of cutting it,
  even with a correct grip. Replace them before the next episode.
* **Faulty can:** a can that dribbles from the spout when held upright, that pours nothing when
  correctly tipped, or that runs dry part way through the three pours. Replace or refill it before
  the next episode.
* **Faulty plant or pot:** a plant whose dead leaves tear instead of cutting, a pot that will not
  stand flat in its saucer, or a saucer that rocks on the table. Replace it before the next episode.
* **A plant or a tool shifts during the episode**, or the plant row turns out to sit outside an arm's
